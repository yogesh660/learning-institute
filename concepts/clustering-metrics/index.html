With no labels, judge clusters by how tight and how separate they are, and choose k with the elbow and silhouette.

01 The idea

Clustering has no ground truth, so internal metrics score the geometry: compact clusters that are far from each other. Inertia measures compactness, silhouette compares cohesion with separation, Davies–Bouldin compares cluster spread with centre distance.

Inertia always falls as $k$ grows (more centres, shorter distances), so we look for the "elbow" where adding clusters stops paying off. Silhouette is self-normalising: for each point, $a$ is the average distance to its own cluster, $b$ to the nearest other cluster, and $s = (b - a)/\max(a, b)$ runs from −1 (wrong cluster) to +1 (well placed).

All of these metrics favour round, convex clusters, so they can rate k-means above a better DBSCAN result on crescent-shaped data. When labels exist for a sample, external metrics like the adjusted Rand index are more trustworthy.

In everyday terms

Judging seating at a wedding: guests should be close to their own table and far from the others.

02 Step by step

  1. Fit the clustering for several k (or settings).
  2. Compute inertia, silhouette and Davies–Bouldin for each.
  3. Look for an elbow in inertia and a peak in silhouette.
  4. Sanity-check clusters with domain knowledge: are they useful?

03 Try it

k-means with the k you choose. Silhouette values per point are shown as a bar chart, sorted within each cluster.

Things to try
  1. Find the k with the highest mean silhouette. Does it match the number of blobs?
  2. Look for points with negative silhouette at the boundary between two clusters.

04 The maths

  1. Silhouette

    $$s(i) = \frac{b(i) - a(i)}{\max\{a(i), b(i)\}}$$

    The overall score is the mean over points. Above about 0.5 is a reasonable structure; below 0.25 is weak.

  2. Davies–Bouldin index

    $$DB = \frac1K\sum_{k=1}^K\max_{j \ne k}\frac{s_k + s_j}{d(\mu_k, \mu_j)}$$

    $s_k$ is the average distance of points to their centre. Lower is better.

05 Key takeaways

  • Never pick k from inertia alone.
  • Internal metrics are biased toward convex clusters.
  • The best clustering is the one that is useful for the decision at hand.

06 In code

python
from sklearn.cluster import KMeans
from sklearn.metrics import silhouette_score, davies_bouldin_score

for k in range(2, 9):
    labels = KMeans(k, n_init=10, random_state=0).fit_predict(X)
    print(k, round(silhouette_score(X, labels), 3), round(davies_bouldin_score(X, labels), 3))

Common pitfalls

  • Computing silhouette with DBSCAN noise points included as a cluster.
  • Treating the silhouette-optimal k as the "true" number of groups.
