Precision, Recall, F1 and the PR Curve

Precision: how many flagged items are right. Recall: how many right items were flagged. F1 balances the two.

01 The idea

Precision is the share of predicted positives that are real; recall is the share of real positives that were found. They pull against each other as the threshold moves. F1 is their harmonic mean, and the precision–recall curve shows the whole trade-off.

A search engine that returns one perfect result has precision 1 and terrible recall. One that returns the whole internet has recall 1 and terrible precision. Most real systems need a point in between, chosen by what each kind of mistake costs.

The harmonic mean in F1 punishes imbalance: precision 1.0 and recall 0.1 give F1 = 0.18, not 0.55. $F_\beta$ lets you weight recall $\beta$ times as much as precision. For rare positives, the area under the PR curve (average precision) is more informative than ROC-AUC.

In everyday terms

A fishing net: precision is how much of your catch is the fish you wanted; recall is how many of those fish in the lake you caught.

02 Step by step

  1. Get predicted probabilities on held-out data.
  2. Sweep the threshold; compute precision and recall at each.
  3. Plot the PR curve; compute average precision.
  4. Choose the threshold that meets your precision or recall target.

04 The maths

  1. Definitions

    $$\text{Precision} = \frac{TP}{TP + FP}, \quad \text{Recall} = \frac{TP}{TP + FN}, \quad F_\beta = (1 + \beta^2)\frac{PR}{\beta^2P + R}$$

    $\beta = 1$ gives F1; $\beta = 2$ weights recall more.

05 Key takeaways

  • A random classifier’s precision equals the positive rate, so PR baselines depend on prevalence.
  • Report precision and recall at the chosen threshold, not just F1.
  • Macro vs micro averaging matters for multi-class problems.

06 In code

python
from sklearn.metrics import precision_recall_curve, average_precision_score, f1_score

proba = model.predict_proba(X_te)[:, 1]
prec, rec, thr = precision_recall_curve(y_te, proba)
print("average precision:", round(average_precision_score(y_te, proba), 3))
ok = prec[:-1] >= 0.9                                     # thresholds giving ≥ 90% precision
print("best recall at ≥90% precision:", rec[:-1][ok].max() if ok.any() else "none")

Common pitfalls

  • Optimising F1 when the business has a clear asymmetric cost.
  • Comparing PR-AUC across datasets with different positive rates.
