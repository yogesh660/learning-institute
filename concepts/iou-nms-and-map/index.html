IoU, NMS and mAP

Object detection extends image classification by predicting both categorical semantics and spatial bounding box coordinates for multiple targets across an image. Evaluating and post-processing object detector outputs depends on three foundational metrics and algorithms: Intersection over Union (IoU) measures geometric bounding box overlap $\frac{|A \cap B|}{|A \cup B|}$; Non-Maximum Suppression (NMS) eliminates redundant overlapping candidate proposals by iteratively suppressing lower-scoring boxes that exceed an IoU overlap threshold with the highest-confidence prediction; and mean Average Precision (mAP) integrates precision-recall curves across classes and IoU thresholds (such as mAP@0.5 and the COCO primary challenge metric mAP@[0.5:0.95]), rigorously penalising false alarms, missed targets, and duplicate detections.

01 The idea

Unlike standard image classification, where a model outputs a single class label per photograph, object detection outputs a variable list of coordinates and confidence scores: "Dog at $[x_1, y_1, x_2, y_2]$ with $92\%$ confidence; Bicycle at $[x_3, y_3, x_4, y_4]$ with $87\%$ confidence." But how close does a predicted box need to be to count as a "hit"? How do you prevent a model from generating twenty slightly shifted boxes over a single cat? And how do you combine localization precision with classification recall into a single benchmark metric? The answers lie in IoU, NMS, and mAP.

Geometric overlap via Intersection over Union (IoU): A predicted bounding box rarely aligns with the ground-truth box down to the exact pixel. Intersection over Union (IoU), also known as the Jaccard Index, quantifies spatial overlap as the area of intersection divided by the area of union: $\text{IoU} = \frac{\text{Area}(B_p \cap B_{gt})}{\text{Area}(B_p \cup B_{gt})}$. IoU is scale-invariant: an overlap score of $0.70$ signifies the identical degree of geometric alignment whether evaluating a tiny pedestrian at distance or a massive freight truck filling the camera frame. An IoU of $1.0$ indicates perfect overlap; an IoU of $0.0$ indicates disjoint boxes.

Eliminating duplicates via Non-Maximum Suppression (NMS): Modern object detectors evaluate thousands of candidate anchor boxes across the spatial grid. A single physical object frequently triggers dozens of adjacent detector heads, producing a cluttered swarm of redundant bounding boxes. Non-Maximum Suppression prunes this crowd greedily: (1) Sort all candidate boxes descending by class confidence score. (2) Select the highest-scoring box $B_{\text{best}}$ and add it to the final detection list. (3) Compare $B_{\text{best}}$ against every remaining box; any box whose IoU with $B_{\text{best}}$ exceeds an overlap threshold $\tau_{\text{NMS}}$ (typically $0.45$ to $0.60$) is suppressed and permanently discarded. (4) Repeat until no candidates remain.

Precision, recall, and the detection matching protocol: To evaluate a detector, predicted boxes must be matched against ground truth. Predictions are sorted by confidence and matched greedily to ground-truth objects. A prediction is classified as a True Positive (TP) if its IoU with an unmatched ground-truth box exceeds an evaluation threshold (e.g. $\text{IoU} \ge 0.50$). If a prediction matches an already-claimed ground-truth object, or if its IoU falls below the threshold, it is labelled a False Positive (FP). Ground-truth objects that receive no matching prediction are False Negatives (FN). Sweeping the confidence threshold from $1.0$ down to $0.0$ traces out a Precision-Recall curve.

From Average Precision (AP) to mAP: Precision-Recall curves are jagged because precision can oscillate as recall increases. Average Precision (AP) computes the area under the interpolated Precision-Recall curve, where the interpolated precision at recall $r$ is taken as the maximum precision found for any recall $r' \ge r$. The mean Average Precision (mAP) averages AP scores across all semantic categories. The modern COCO evaluation benchmark averages mAP across ten IoU overlap thresholds spanning $0.50$ to $0.95$ in steps of $0.05$ (mAP@[0.5:0.95]), demanding both coarse detection capability and sub-pixel localization accuracy.

In everyday terms

Imagine an archery competition. Calculating IoU is like measuring how much of your arrow's broad arrowhead overlaps the bullseye ring painted on the target: if your arrow clips the edge, the overlap is 10%; if it strikes dead centre, the overlap is 90%. Non-Maximum Suppression is like a scoring referee resolving an archer who fires a volley of ten tightly grouped arrows at the same bullseye: the referee awards points for the single best shot in the cluster and pulls the other nine arrows out of the board so they don't count as ten separate bullseyes. Mean Average Precision (mAP) is like the tournament decathlon leaderboard: it scores how consistently an archer hits targets as distance increases, averaging performance across different bow disciplines (classes) and across both lenient target rings (IoU 0.5) and strict pin-point bullseyes (IoU 0.95).

03 The maths

  1. Intersection over Union (Jaccard Index)

    \operatorname{IoU}(A, B) = \frac{|A \cap B|}{|A \cup B|} = \frac{|A \cap B|}{|A| + |B| - |A \cap B|} \in [0, 1]

    Here $|A|$ and $|B|$ denote the 2D spatial areas of bounding boxes $A$ and $B$. IoU is strictly bounded between $0$ (completely disjoint boxes) and $1$ (exact geometric identity).

  2. Precision, Recall, and Interpolated Precision

    P = \frac{\text{TP}}{\text{TP} + \text{FP}}, \quad R = \frac{\text{TP}}{\text{TP} + \text{FN}}, \quad P_{\text{interp}}(r) = \max_{r' \ge r} P(r')

    Precision measures the proportion of predicted detections that are authentic targets. Recall measures the proportion of total ground-truth targets successfully found. Replacing $P(r)$ with $P_{\text{interp}}(r)$ creates a monotonically non-increasing envelope that eliminates noise spikes from individual prediction rankings.

  3. COCO mean Average Precision (mAP@[0.50:0.95])

    \text{mAP} = \frac{1}{|C|} \sum_{c \in C} \left( \frac{1}{10} \sum_{\tau \in \{0.50, 0.55, \dots, 0.95\}} \text{AP}_{c, \tau} \right)

    The standard MS COCO benchmark computes Average Precision for each class $c \in C$ across ten IoU match thresholds $\tau \in [0.50, 0.95]$ in increments of $0.05$. Averaging across all IoU levels and classes yields the primary challenge metric mAP.

04 Key takeaways

  • IoU measures scale-invariant spatial overlap as the ratio of intersection area to union area.
  • Non-Maximum Suppression (NMS) greedily eliminates redundant overlapping candidate boxes based on confidence rankings and IoU overlap thresholds.
  • In object detection evaluation, each ground-truth object can be matched at most once; subsequent duplicate detections are penalised as False Positives.
  • Average Precision (AP) measures the area under the interpolated Precision-Recall curve for a single semantic class.
  • COCO mAP averages AP across multiple IoU thresholds ($0.50$ to $0.95$) and all object classes, rewarding precise bounding box localization.

05 In code

python
import numpy as np

# Vectorized Intersection over Union (IoU) calculation
def compute_iou(box1, boxes):
    # box1: [x1, y1, x2, y2]
    # boxes: [[x1, y1, x2, y2], ...] shape (N, 4)
    x1 = np.maximum(box1[0], boxes[:, 0])
    y1 = np.maximum(box1[1], boxes[:, 1])
    x2 = np.minimum(box1[2], boxes[:, 2])
    y2 = np.minimum(box1[3], boxes[:, 3])
    
    inter_w = np.maximum(0.0, x2 - x1)
    inter_h = np.maximum(0.0, y2 - y1)
    inter_area = inter_w * inter_h
    
    area1 = (box1[2] - box1[0]) * (box1[3] - box1[1])
    area2 = (boxes[:, 2] - boxes[:, 0]) * (boxes[:, 3] - boxes[:, 1])
    union_area = area1 + area2 - inter_area
    
    return inter_area / np.maximum(union_area, 1e-6)

# 1. Greedy Non-Maximum Suppression (NMS)
def nms(boxes, scores, iou_threshold=0.5):
    order = np.argsort(scores)[::-1]
    keep = []
    
    while len(order) > 0:
        idx = order[0]
        keep.append(idx)
        if len(order) == 1:
            break
            
        ious = compute_iou(boxes[idx], boxes[order[1:]])
        # Retain only boxes whose IoU is below the suppression threshold
        survivors = np.where(ious <= iou_threshold)[0]
        order = order[survivors + 1]
        
    return keep

# Simulate 5 candidate boxes (e.g. 3 overlapping boxes on one object, 2 on another)
candidate_boxes = np.array([
    [50.0,  50.0,  150.0, 150.0],  # Box A (top confidence: 0.95)
    [52.0,  48.0,  155.0, 152.0],  # Box A overlap 1 (conf: 0.88)
    [48.0,  55.0,  145.0, 148.0],  # Box A overlap 2 (conf: 0.72)
    [200.0, 200.0, 300.0, 300.0],  # Box B (top confidence: 0.91)
    [205.0, 195.0, 310.0, 305.0],  # Box B overlap 1 (conf: 0.65)
], dtype=np.float32)

candidate_scores = np.array([0.95, 0.88, 0.72, 0.91, 0.65], dtype=np.float32)

kept_indices = nms(candidate_boxes, candidate_scores, iou_threshold=0.5)
print(f"Total candidate proposals: {len(candidate_boxes)}")
print(f"Indices kept after NMS:    {kept_indices}")
print(f"Selected boxes (one per true target):\n{candidate_boxes[kept_indices]}")

# 2. Precision-Recall curve and 11-point interpolated Average Precision (AP)
# Simulated matching results (sorted by confidence)
tp = np.array([1, 1, 0, 1, 0, 1, 0, 0, 1, 0], dtype=np.float32)
fp = np.array([0, 0, 1, 0, 1, 0, 1, 1, 0, 1], dtype=np.float32)
num_ground_truth = 6

cum_tp = np.cumsum(tp)
cum_fp = np.cumsum(fp)
recalls = cum_tp / num_ground_truth
precisions = cum_tp / (cum_tp + cum_fp)

# 11-point interpolation (recall thresholds: 0.0, 0.1, ..., 1.0)
ap_11 = 0.0
for r_thresh in np.linspace(0.0, 1.0, 11):
    prec_candidates = precisions[recalls >= r_thresh]
    p_interp = np.max(prec_candidates) if len(prec_candidates) > 0 else 0.0
    ap_11 += p_interp / 11.0

print(f"\nDetection AP evaluation (11-point interpolated):")
print(f"Total True Positives found: {cum_tp[-1]:.0f} / {num_ground_truth} GT targets")
print(f"Final Recall:               {recalls[-1]:.3f}")
print(f"Calculated AP@0.5:          {ap_11:.4f}")

Common pitfalls

  • Double-counting ground truth matches: Allowing multiple predictions to claim the same ground-truth object artificially inflates precision; subsequent overlapping predictions must be counted as False Positives.
  • Setting NMS threshold too low: Setting $\tau_{\text{NMS}} < 0.30$ causes NMS to inadvertently suppress distinct adjacent objects in crowded scenes (such as people standing close together in a queue).
  • Running class-agnostic NMS when objects can overlap: Applying NMS across all classes simultaneously can cause a high-confidence "person" prediction to suppress a valid overlapping "bicycle" prediction; NMS should normally be evaluated per-class.
  • Evaluating mAP without checking confidence ranking: Failing to sort predictions descending by confidence before assigning TP and FP labels completely invalidates the Precision-Recall curve.
  • Assuming IoU is differentiable when disjoint: Standard IoU gradient is zero whenever boxes do not overlap ($\text{IoU}=0$), which prevents gradient flow during training; modern detectors use generalised IoU (GIoU), Distance-IoU (DIoU), or Complete-IoU (CIoU) loss.

06 Check yourself

Q1Why is Intersection over Union (IoU) preferred over absolute pixel Euclidean distance when measuring bounding box localization error?

Why: A 10-pixel error on a 20-pixel object represents a catastrophic $50\%$ displacement, whereas a 10-pixel error on a 500-pixel object is negligible. Dividing intersection by union normalises for target scale, providing a scale-invariant metric in $[0, 1]$.

Q2In Non-Maximum Suppression (NMS), why are candidate boxes sorted descending by confidence score before evaluating overlap?

Why: Greedy NMS works by selecting the highest-confidence prediction as the primary reference box for a detected object and then pruning all lower-scoring candidate boxes that overlap significantly with it.

Q3When evaluating detections against ground truth to construct a Precision-Recall curve, what happens if two predicted boxes both overlap a single ground-truth object above the IoU threshold?

Why: Each ground-truth object can be matched at most once. The first (highest-scoring) matching prediction claims the True Positive; any subsequent duplicate predictions for that same object are penalised as False Positives to deter duplicate clutter.

Q4How does the primary COCO benchmark metric (mAP@[0.50:0.95]) differ from the classical PASCAL VOC metric (mAP@0.50)?

Why: While PASCAL VOC accepted any detection with $\text{IoU} \ge 0.50$, the COCO benchmark averages across $0.50, 0.55, \dots, 0.95$. A detector with loose, sloppy boxes scores poorly under COCO mAP, pushing models to predict tight, exact boundaries.
