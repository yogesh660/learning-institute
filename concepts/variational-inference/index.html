Variational Inference

01 The idea

Exact Bayesian posterior calculation requires computing a marginal likelihood integral over latent variables that is intractable for all but conjugate models. Variational inference circumvents numerical integration by recasting inference as an optimisation problem.

In Bayesian modelling, we observe data $x$ generated via unobserved latent variables $z$ according to a joint distribution $p(x, z) = p(x \mid z) p(z)$. The central goal is evaluating the posterior distribution $p(z \mid x) = p(x, z) / p(x)$. However, the marginal likelihood (evidence) $p(x) = \int p(x, z) dz$ is frequently an intractable high-dimensional integral when priors and likelihoods are non-conjugate, as in deep latent variable models or complex hierarchical Bayesian networks.

Markov chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) tackles this intractability by constructing a Markov chain whose stationary distribution matches the exact posterior. While asymptotically exact, MCMC explores state spaces slowly and scales poorly to millions of observations or real-time latent inferences. Variational inference (VI) offers an alternative paradigm: instead of sampling, approximate the posterior using continuous optimisation.

VI posits a tractable family of distributions over latent variables $\mathcal{Q} = \{q_\theta(z)\}$ parameterised by variational parameters $\theta$. It then searches within $\mathcal{Q}$ for the distribution $q^*(z)$ that minimises the Kullback–Leibler divergence $D_{\mathrm{KL}}(q_\theta(z) \parallel p(z \mid x))$ to the true posterior. Because the true posterior $p(z \mid x)$ is unknown, we cannot minimise this divergence directly. Fortunately, decomposing the log marginal evidence reveals that maximising the Evidence Lower Bound (ELBO) is mathematically identical to minimising this divergence.

The choice of family $\mathcal{Q}$ determines both fidelity and computational tractability. The classic mean-field approximation assumes latent variables factorise completely: $q(z) = \prod_{j=1}^M q_j(z_j)$. Under this structure, Coordinate Ascent Variational Inference (CAVI) iteratively updates each variational factor in closed form. In modern deep learning, variational autoencoders (VAEs) employ stochastic gradient variational inference, using amortised neural networks and the reparameterisation trick to optimise the ELBO via standard backpropagation.

In everyday terms

Imagine sculpting a statue out of a block of marble to match a phantom figure standing behind a frosted glass screen. You cannot touch or measure the phantom directly (the intractable posterior), but every step closer your chisel sculpts the marble against a shadow projection (the joint likelihood and prior) raises the total illumination in the studio (the evidence lower bound). When illumination reaches its peak, your marble sculpture is the closest possible physical approximation within your sculpting toolset.

03 The maths

  1. Evidence and KL Decomposition

    \log p(x) = \mathbb{E}_{q(z)}\left[\log \frac{p(x, z)}{q(z)}\right] + D_{\mathrm{KL}}(q(z) \parallel p(z \mid x)) = \mathrm{ELBO}(q) + D_{\mathrm{KL}}(q \parallel p)

    Because the KL divergence is non-negative and equals zero if and only if q(z) = p(z|x), the ELBO serves as a strict mathematical lower bound on the log evidence log p(x). Maximising the ELBO with respect to q directly minimises KL divergence to the exact posterior.

  2. ELBO Components: Reconstruction vs Regularisation

    \mathrm{ELBO}(q) = \mathbb{E}_{q(z)}[\log p(x \mid z)] - D_{\mathrm{KL}}(q(z) \parallel p(z))

    Rewriting the ELBO decomposes the objective into an expected log-likelihood term (rewarding variational states that reconstruct observed data well) and a prior KL penalty (regularising q(z) toward prior p(z)).

  3. Mean-Field Coordinate Ascent (CAVI)

    \log q_j^*(z_j) = \mathbb{E}_{-j}[\log p(x, z_j, z_{-j})] + \text{const}

    For each latent variable j, the optimal mean-field factor q_j*(z_j) is proportional to the exponential of the expected complete-data log-likelihood conditioned on all remaining factors -j.

04 Key takeaways

  • Transforms Bayesian inference from intractable numerical integration into continuous optimisation over distribution parameters.
  • The Evidence Lower Bound (ELBO) satisfies ELBO(q) = log p(x) - D_KL(q || p) <= log p(x); maximising it minimises divergence to the true posterior.
  • Mean-field variational inference factorises latent variables independently, enabling coordinate ascent (CAVI) in conditionally conjugate models.
  • Underestimates posterior variance: because reverse KL heavily penalises places where q > 0 but p approx 0, VI fits tightly into the dominant mode rather than covering the whole spread.
  • Stochastic VI and the reparameterisation trick allow VI to scale to massive datasets and power variational autoencoders (VAEs).

05 In code

python
import numpy as np

# Coordinate Ascent Variational Inference (CAVI) for 1D Gaussian Mixture
# Model: x_i ~ N(mu_{c_i}, 1), prior mu_k ~ N(0, sigma_0^2), equal cluster weights
np.random.seed(42)

# Generate synthetic two-component mixture
n_samples = 300
true_mus = np.array([-2.5, 3.0])
assignments = np.random.binomial(1, 0.5, size=n_samples)
x = np.where(assignments == 0, np.random.normal(true_mus[0], 1.0, n_samples),
                               np.random.normal(true_mus[1], 1.0, n_samples))

K = 2
sigma0_sq = 10.0  # Prior variance on cluster means

# Initialise variational parameters:
# q(mu_k) = N(m_k, s2_k), q(c_i) = Categorical(phi_i)
m = np.random.normal(0, 1, size=K)
s2 = np.ones(K)
phi = np.ones((n_samples, K)) / K

def compute_elbo(x, m, s2, phi, sigma0_sq):
    elbo = 0.0
    for k in range(K):
        # Prior on mu_k
        elbo += -0.5 * np.log(2 * np.pi * sigma0_sq) - (m[k]**2 + s2[k]) / (2 * sigma0_sq)
        # Entropy of q(mu_k)
        elbo += 0.5 * np.log(2 * np.pi * np.e * s2[k])
    
    # Expected likelihood and cluster entropy
    for i in range(n_samples):
        for k in range(K):
            if phi[i, k] > 1e-12:
                exp_log_lik = -0.5 * np.log(2 * np.pi) - 0.5 * ((x[i] - m[k])**2 + s2[k])
                elbo += phi[i, k] * (exp_log_lik - np.log(phi[i, k]) - np.log(K))
    return elbo

# Coordinate Ascent iterations
n_iters = 25
elbo_history = []

for it in range(n_iters):
    # 1. Update cluster responsibilities phi_ik
    for i in range(n_samples):
        log_rho = np.zeros(K)
        for k in range(K):
            log_rho[k] = -0.5 * ((x[i] - m[k])**2 + s2[k]) - np.log(K)
        # Softmax normalisation for stability
        rho = np.exp(log_rho - np.max(log_rho))
        phi[i] = rho / np.sum(rho)
        
    # 2. Update variational parameters for cluster means m_k and s2_k
    for k in range(K):
        sum_phi = np.sum(phi[:, k])
        s2[k] = 1.0 / (1.0 / sigma0_sq + sum_phi)
        m[k] = s2[k] * np.sum(phi[:, k] * x)
        
    elbo = compute_elbo(x, m, s2, phi, sigma0_sq)
    elbo_history.append(elbo)

print(f"True cluster means: {true_mus}")
print(f"Variational posterior means: {np.sort(m).round(3)}")
print(f"Variational posterior variances: {s2[np.argsort(m)].round(4)}")
print(f"Initial ELBO: {elbo_history[0]:.2f} -> Final ELBO: {elbo_history[-1]:.2f}")
print(f"ELBO strictly non-decreasing: {np.all(np.diff(elbo_history) >= -1e-7)}")

Common pitfalls

  • Zero-forcing variance underestimation: Reverse KL forces q(z) to remain strictly within high-density regions of p(z|x), underestimating true parameter uncertainty and ignoring secondary modes.
  • Mean-field correlation blindness: Assuming complete factorisation q(z) = \prod q_j(z_j) forces all covariance between latent variables to zero, potentially corrupting inference when latents are strongly coupled.
  • Local optima: Unlike MCMC which theoretically visits the whole support asymptotically, gradient ascent on the ELBO can get trapped in local optima or bad saddle points.

06 Check yourself

Q1Why does maximising the Evidence Lower Bound (ELBO) minimise the KL divergence D_KL(q(z) || p(z|x))?

Why: The fundamental decomposition is log p(x) = ELBO(q) + D_KL(q || p(z|x)). Since log p(x) does not depend on variational distribution q, increasing ELBO directly forces D_KL down.

Q2What is a major qualitative difference between the posterior approximation from mean-field VI compared to MCMC?

Why: Because reverse KL D_KL(q || p) is zero-forcing and the mean-field assumption forces factorisation q(z) = \prod q_j(z_j), VI fits tightly inside the primary mode and zeroes out off-diagonal posterior covariances.
