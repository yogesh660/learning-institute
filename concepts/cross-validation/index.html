Cross-Validation

Estimate how a model will do on new data by rotating which part of the data is held out. The honest way to tune.

01 The idea

K-fold cross-validation splits the data into $K$ folds, trains on $K-1$ and validates on the remaining one, rotating through all $K$. The average score estimates performance on unseen data; the spread tells you how much to trust it.

One train/validation split gives one noisy number: a lucky split flatters the model. Rotating the hold-out uses every row for validation exactly once and averages the luck away. With $K = 5$ or $10$, each model trains on 80–90% of the data, so the estimate is only slightly pessimistic.

The split must mimic how the model will be used. Stratify by class for imbalanced classification. Group by entity (customer, patient) when rows from one entity are correlated. Respect time order for forecasting: never train on the future.

In everyday terms

Instead of one exam, a student sits five exams, each made of questions they did not practise on, and the average grade is recorded.

02 Step by step

  1. Hold out a final test set and do not touch it.
  2. Split the rest into $K$ folds (stratified, grouped or time-ordered as appropriate).
  3. For each fold: fit everything (scaling, selection, the model) on the other folds, score on this one.
  4. Report the mean and standard deviation; pick hyperparameters by the mean.
  5. Refit on all non-test data and evaluate once on the test set.

04 The maths

  1. K-fold estimate

    $$\widehat{\text{Err}}_{\text{CV}} = \frac1K\sum_{k=1}^K\frac{1}{|F_k|}\sum_{i \in F_k}\ell\big(y_i, \hat f^{(-k)}(x_i)\big)$$

    $\hat f^{(-k)}$ is trained without fold $F_k$.

05 Key takeaways

  • Every preprocessing step that learns from data must happen inside the CV loop (use pipelines).
  • Nested CV (tuning inside, evaluation outside) is needed for an unbiased estimate of a tuned model.
  • Time series need forward-chaining splits.

06 In code

python
from sklearn.model_selection import cross_validate, StratifiedKFold, TimeSeriesSplit, GroupKFold
from sklearn.pipeline import make_pipeline
from sklearn.preprocessing import StandardScaler
from sklearn.linear_model import LogisticRegression

model = make_pipeline(StandardScaler(), LogisticRegression(max_iter=1000))   # scaling inside CV
cv = StratifiedKFold(n_splits=5, shuffle=True, random_state=0)
scores = cross_validate(model, X, y, cv=cv, scoring=["accuracy", "roc_auc"])
print({k: v.mean().round(3) for k, v in scores.items() if k.startswith("test_")})

Common pitfalls

  • Scaling or selecting features on all data before CV: leakage.
  • Shuffled K-fold on time series.
  • Random splits when the same customer appears in many rows.

07 Check yourself

Q1Why put the scaler inside the cross-validation pipeline?

Why: Fitting the scaler on all data leaks validation information.

Q2For forecasting next month’s sales, which split is right?

Why: Training must only use data from before the validation period.

Q3What is Stratified $K$-fold cross-validation, and when is it essential?

Why: In imbalanced datasets (e.g. 5% positive class), standard random splitting can produce folds with almost zero positive examples. Stratification guarantees representative class proportions across all folds.

Q4What is Group $K$-fold cross-validation, and what kind of leakage does it prevent?

Why: If a patient has 20 medical records scattered across both train and validation folds, the model memorises patient-specific quirks rather than generalising pathology. GroupKFold keeps entire groups intact in either train or test.

Q5Why is Nested Cross-Validation used when tuning hyperparameters?

Why: If you tune hyperparameters on the same CV folds used to report performance, you pick the configuration that got lucky on those folds, producing an optimistically biased score. Nested CV decouples tuning from evaluation.

Q6What is the main theoretical drawback of Leave-One-Out Cross-Validation (LOOCV, where $K = n$) compared to 5-fold or 10-fold CV?

Why: In LOOCV, each of the $n$ models is trained on $(n-1)/n$ of the data. Because the models are nearly identical, their test errors are highly correlated. The variance of the mean of correlated variables is higher than that of less correlated folds in 10-fold CV.

Q7After completing $K$-fold cross-validation and choosing the optimal hyperparameters, what is the standard next step before deploying the model?

Why: Cross-validation serves to evaluate the modelling process and select hyperparameters. Once chosen, refitting on 100% of the non-test data uses all available information, producing the best final model for production.

Q8What is the "one-standard-error rule" (1-SE rule) often used when selecting hyperparameters in cross-validation?

Why: The best numerical score often overfits fold noise slightly. The 1-SE rule (Breiman et al.) prefers the simpler, more regularised model whose performance is statistically indistinguishable from the absolute peak.

Q9Why does applying a standard shuffled $K$-fold cross-validation to chronological time series data (e.g. daily stock prices) produce invalid results?

Why: Autocorrelated time series let future points leak information backward. A model that knows tomorrow's price can trivially interpolate today's price. Rolling-origin / forward-chaining splits are required.

Q10In a 5-fold cross-validation on a regression dataset of $n = 500$ samples, how many samples are in the training set and validation set for each fold?

Why: Each fold holds out $1/K = 1/5 = 20\%$ of the data for validation ($500 / 5 = 100$ samples) and trains on the remaining $(K-1)/K = 4/5 = 80\%$ ($4 \times 100 = 400$ samples).

Q11A 5-fold cross-validation produces accuracy scores across the five folds of: $[0.80, 0.82, 0.85, 0.84, 0.89]$. What are the mean cross-validation accuracy and the sample variance $s^2 = \frac{1}{K-1}\sum (x_i - \bar{x})^2$?

Why: Sum $= 0.80 + 0.82 + 0.85 + 0.84 + 0.89 = 4.20$. Mean $\bar{x} = 4.20 / 5 = 0.840$. Deviations: $[-0.04, -0.02, +0.01, 0.00, +0.05]$. Squared deviations: $[0.0016, 0.0004, 0.0001, 0.0000, 0.0025]$. Sum of squared deviations $= 0.0046$. Sample variance $s^2 = 0.0046 / (5 - 1) = 0.0046 / 4 = 0.00115$.

Q12You perform nested cross-validation with 5 outer folds and 4 inner folds to evaluate a grid of 6 hyperparameter combinations. How many total model fits are executed across the entire nested cross-validation process?

Why: For each of the 5 outer folds, the inner cross-validation evaluates all 6 hyperparameter settings across 4 inner folds ($6 \times 4 = 24$ fits). Across all 5 outer folds, this requires $5 \times 24 = 120$ inner fits.
