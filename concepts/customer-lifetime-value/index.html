Customer Lifetime Value (CLV): Buy 'Til You Die Models and Expected Value

Estimating future customer financial value in contractual and non-contractual settings using Pareto/NBD, BG/NBD, and Gamma-Gamma spend models.

01 The idea

Customer Lifetime Value (CLV) is the definitive economic compass of customer-centric businesses, determining how much a company can afford to spend on customer acquisition (CAC), which customer segments merit bespoke VIP service, and how corporate equity should be valued. In non-contractual settings (e.g. e-commerce, hospitality), customers never notify a company when they leave—they simply stop buying. "Buy 'Til You Die" (BTYD) models probabilistically uncouple transaction frequency from the unobserved latent churn process.

In contractual settings (SaaS, telecom), churn is directly observed via subscription cancellations, making CLV tractable through retention rate discount formulas: $\mathrm{CLV} = \mathrm{Margin} \times \frac{r}{1 + d - r}$.

In non-contractual settings (e-commerce, retail), transactions occur at random intervals, and customer attrition is silent and unobserved. If a customer made 5 purchases last year and none in the last 6 months, are they dormant or dead?

BTYD models (such as Pareto/NBD and the BG/NBD model by Fader et al.) capture this using two intertwined latent processes: while "alive", a customer makes repeat purchases according to a Poisson process with rate $\lambda_i$; after each transaction, the customer has a probability $p_i$ of permanently dropping out (dying).

Individual purchase rates $\lambda_i$ and dropout probabilities $p_i$ vary across the customer base according to Gamma and Beta prior distributions, capturing customer heterogeneity. The separate Gamma-Gamma model estimates average monetary transaction value, completing the expected financial CLV calculation.

In everyday terms

Imagine a neighbourhood coffee shop. Regular patrons visit three times a week, but occasionally someone stops showing up. Did they move to another city (churned), or did they go on a three-week holiday and will return next week? BTYD probabilistic models act like an experienced barista who knows that for a customer who usually visits daily, a 10-day silence likely means they have moved away, whereas for a once-a-month customer, a 10-day gap is entirely normal.

02 Step by step

  1. Classify business regime: Determine whether business model is contractual (subscription) or non-contractual (retail/e-commerce).
  2. Aggregate customer RFM summary: For each customer, calculate frequency $x$ (number of repeat transactions), recency $t_x$ (time of last transaction), and total observation time $T$.
  3. Fit BG/NBD model: Estimate latent purchase rate parameters $(r, \alpha)$ and dropout parameters $(a, b)$ via maximum likelihood estimation.
  4. Estimate latent alive probability: Compute $P(\text{alive}_i \mid x_i, t_{x,i}, T_i)$ to identify which customers are actively engaged vs dormant.
  5. Fit Gamma-Gamma monetary model: Model average transaction values to estimate expected spend per transaction conditional on customer history.
  6. Project discounted net present value: Multiply projected transaction counts over horizon $H$ by expected transaction margin, applying financial discount rate $d$.

03 The maths

  1. Contractual Infinite-Horizon CLV

    \mathrm{CLV} = \sum_{t=1}^\infty \frac{M \cdot r^t}{(1 + d)^t} = M \cdot \frac{r}{1 + d - r}

    Calculates the net present value of expected cash margins $M$ under constant retention rate $r$ and annual discount rate $d$.

  2. BG/NBD Latent State Likelihood

    L(r, \alpha, a, b \mid x, t_x, T) = A_1 \cdot A_2 \cdot \left( A_3 + \mathbb{I}(x > 0) \cdot A_4 \right)

    Formulates joint likelihood over observed repeat purchases $x$, recency $t_x$, and tenure $T$, integrating out unobserved customer death times.

  3. BG/NBD Probability of Being Alive

    P(\text{alive} \mid x, t_x, T) = \left( 1 + \frac{a}{b + x} \left( \frac{\alpha + T}{\alpha + t_x} \right)^{r + x} \right)^{-1}

    Evaluates the probability that a customer remains active given their elapsed recency $(T - t_x)$ relative to their historical transaction frequency $x$.

04 Key takeaways

  • CLV quantifies the total financial net present value a customer brings throughout their entire commercial relationship.
  • Contractual businesses have explicit cancellations, modelled via retention discount formulas.
  • Non-contractual businesses feature unobserved churn, requiring Buy 'Til You Die (BTYD / BG-NBD) models.
  • Expected monetary value per transaction is typically modelled independently using a Gamma-Gamma distribution.

05 In code

python
import numpy as np

def contractual_clv(monthly_margin: float, monthly_retention: float, annual_discount: float) -> float:
    """Calculate Net Present Value CLV for subscription business."""
    # Convert annual discount rate to monthly
    monthly_discount = (1.0 + annual_discount) ** (1.0 / 12.0) - 1.0
    return monthly_margin * (monthly_retention / (1.0 + monthly_discount - monthly_retention))

def simple_p_alive_heuristic(recency: float, tenure: float, frequency: int) -> float:
    """Heuristic approximation of probability of being alive in non-contractual setting."""
    if frequency == 0:
        return 0.5
    avg_inter_arrival = recency / frequency
    elapsed_since_last = tenure - recency
    # Decay probability based on how many expected purchase cycles have elapsed since last seen
    cycles_missed = elapsed_since_last / max(avg_inter_arrival, 1.0)
    return float(1.0 / (1.0 + np.exp(cycles_missed - 2.0)))

# Example 1: Contractual SaaS Customer ($100/mo margin, 95% monthly retention, 10% discount)
saas_clv = contractual_clv(monthly_margin=100.0, monthly_retention=0.95, annual_discount=0.10)
print(f"Contractual SaaS Expected CLV: £{saas_clv:.2f}")

# Example 2: E-commerce Latent P(Alive)
# Customer A bought 10 times in 100 days, last bought 5 days ago (tenure 105)
p_alive_a = simple_p_alive_heuristic(recency=100.0, tenure=105.0, frequency=10)
# Customer B bought 10 times in 100 days, last bought 200 days ago (tenure 300)
p_alive_b = simple_p_alive_heuristic(recency=100.0, tenure=300.0, frequency=10)

print(f"Customer A (5 days silent):   P(Alive) = {p_alive_a * 100:.1f}%")
print(f"Customer B (200 days silent): P(Alive) = {p_alive_b * 100:.1f}%")

Common pitfalls

  • Applying historical average spend blindly: Assuming a customer who spent £1,000 in their first month will continue spending £1,000 every month ignores natural regression to the mean.
  • Confusing revenue with gross margin: Calculating CLV on gross merchandise value (GMV) rather than contribution margin leads to catastrophic overspending on customer acquisition.
  • Ignoring discounting: For long horizons (3–5 years), failing to discount future cash flows overstates present financial value.

06 Check yourself

Q1Why are standard classification models inadequate for predicting customer churn and CLV in non-contractual e-commerce?

Why: In non-contractual commerce, there is no cancellation notice. An unobserved drop-off must be probabilistically inferred from transaction frequency and recency.

Q2In the BG/NBD model, what happens to the estimated $P(\text{alive})$ as the time elapsed since a customer's last purchase $(T - t_x)$ grows much larger than their typical inter-purchase interval?

Why: The longer a customer remains silent relative to their historical purchasing rhythm, the higher the probability that they have permanently dropped out.

Q3What is the fundamental business purpose of calculating Customer Lifetime Value (CLV)?

Why: CLV establishes the economic upper bound for Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC) and dictates optimal budget allocation for customer loyalty and VIP services.
