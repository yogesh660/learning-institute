CNN architecture tour: LeNet to ResNet to EfficientNet

The evolution of convolutional neural networks charts a thirty-year trajectory of overcoming vanishing gradients, parameter explosion, and computational bottlenecks. From Yann LeCun's LeNet-5 (1998) and Alex Krizhevsky's AlexNet (2012) to VGG's modular $3 \times 3$ factorisation, GoogLeNet's multi-scale Inception branches, He et al.'s revolutionary ResNet identity skip connections ($\mathbf{x} + \mathcal{F}(\mathbf{x})$), DenseNet's dense connectivity, and Tan & Le's EfficientNet compound scaling ($d, w, r$), this architectural progression reveals the fundamental design principles that power deep visual representation learning.

01 The idea

In 1998, a state-of-the-art convolutional network could process $32 \times 32$ greyscale cheques using 60,000 parameters. By 2012, AlexNet ignited the modern deep learning revolution by classifying 1.2 million high-resolution ImageNet photographs across dual GPUs using 60 million parameters. Yet training networks beyond twenty layers caused performance to degrade rapidly—not from overfitting, but from vanishing gradients. Overcoming this barrier demanded a series of architectural breakthroughs that reshaped how representations flow through deep computation graphs.

The pioneer era: LeNet and AlexNet: Yann LeCun established the core paradigm of modern computer vision in LeNet-5: alternating convolutional layers (for translation-equivariant local feature extraction) and subsampling layers (for spatial pooling), followed by fully connected classification heads. In 2012, AlexNet scaled this blueprint to ImageNet by replacing sigmoid non-linearities with Rectified Linear Units (ReLU) to avert gradient saturation, employing Dropout to combat overfitting, and implementing parallel split-convolution layers across two GPUs. AlexNet slashed the ImageNet top-5 error rate from $26\%$ to $16\%$, proving the viability of deep learning.

Factorisation and multi-scale branching: VGG and Inception: VGG (2014) introduced mathematical elegance and architectural discipline: replacing heterogeneous filter sizes ($11 \times 11, 5 \times 5$) with homogeneous stacks of $3 \times 3$ convolutions. VGG demonstrated that depth itself is a critical dimension of representational power. Simultaneously, GoogLeNet (Inception-v1) tackled computational efficiency by processing features across parallel multi-scale branches ($1 \times 1, 3 \times 3, 5 \times 5$, and max-pooling), introducing $1 \times 1$ "bottleneck" convolutions to project channel dimensions down prior to expensive spatial convolutions.

The residual revolution: ResNet and DenseNet: As researchers tried stacking VGG layers to 30 or 50 tiers, training error degraded—a fundamental optimisation failure. In 2015, Kaiming He et al. introduced ResNet (Residual Networks), transforming the layer learning objective from fitting an underlying mapping $\mathcal{H}(\mathbf{x})$ directly to fitting a residual function $\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{x}) = \mathcal{H}(\mathbf{x}) - \mathbf{x}$. By adding identity shortcut skip connections $\mathbf{y} = \mathbf{x} + \mathcal{F}(\mathbf{x})$, gradients can flow unimpeded directly from the loss back to early layers via the identity operator. ResNet trained models of 152 layers—and even 1,202 layers—winning ImageNet with a superhuman $3.57\%$ error rate. DenseNet pushed this logic further by concatenating all preceding feature maps directly into every subsequent layer.

Principled scaling: EfficientNet: After ResNet, practitioners scaled networks arbitrarily by making them deeper (more layers), wider (more channels), or higher resolution (larger input images). In 2019, Mingxing Tan and Quoc Le demonstrated that scaling only one dimension yields diminishing returns because receptive fields, channel capacities, and spatial pixel details must balance. EfficientNet introduced "compound scaling": using a compound coefficient $\phi$ to scale depth, width, and resolution simultaneously via fixed power ratios ($\alpha^\phi, \beta^\phi, \gamma^\phi$). Paired with lightweight inverted residual bottleneck blocks (MBConv), EfficientNet achieved state-of-the-art accuracy with up to $8.4\times$ fewer parameters and $6.1\times$ fewer FLOPs than previous architectures.

In everyday terms

Think of designing a deep transport railway network across a vast continent. LeNet built the first small regional tram line. AlexNet proved that an industrial steam locomotive could traverse massive mountain ranges, though it burned enormous coal and belched smoke (millions of dense fully connected weights). VGG standardised the track gauges using uniform steel segments ($3 \times 3$ blocks), proving that longer trains carry more cargo. ResNet's identity skip connection was the invention of express bypass tracks: rather than forcing every single package to be unloaded and reloaded at every tiny local village station (where goods get lost or delayed by gradient degradation), express bypass tracks allow trains to shoot uninterrupted across hundreds of kilometres directly to destination terminals, dropping off local freight along the way. EfficientNet acts like modern transport logistics optimisation: rather than blindly making trains twice as long without upgrading station platforms or track width, it balances train length, carriage width, and track gauge in mathematical harmony.

03 The maths

  1. ResNet residual block formulation and gradient highway

    \mathbf{y} = \mathcal{F}(\mathbf{x}, \{W_i\}) + \mathbf{x} \implies \frac{\partial \mathcal{E}}{\partial \mathbf{x}} = \frac{\partial \mathcal{E}}{\partial \mathbf{y}} \left(\frac{\partial \mathcal{F}}{\partial \mathbf{x}} + I\right)

    The gradient of the loss $\mathcal{E}$ with respect to input $\mathbf{x}$ contains the additive term $\frac{\partial \mathcal{E}}{\partial \mathbf{y}} \cdot I$. Even if the Jacobian term $\frac{\partial \mathcal{F}}{\partial \mathbf{x}}$ vanishes or approaches zero across dozens of layers, the identity operator $I$ guarantees that gradients flow cleanly back to shallow layers without vanishing.

  2. Depthwise separable convolution parameter reduction

    \frac{|\Theta_{\text{depthwise-separable}}|}{|\Theta_{\text{standard}}|} = \frac{k^2 \cdot C_{\text{in}} + C_{\text{in}} \cdot C_{\text{out}}}{k^2 \cdot C_{\text{in}} \cdot C_{\text{out}}} = \frac{1}{C_{\text{out}}} + \frac{1}{k^2}

    A standard convolution requires $k^2 \cdot C_{\text{in}} \cdot C_{\text{out}}$ weights. Factoring into a depthwise convolution ($k^2 \cdot C_{\text{in}}$) followed by a $1 \times 1$ pointwise projection ($C_{\text{in}} \cdot C_{\text{out}}$) reduces computational cost and parameter storage by approximately $8\times$ to $9\times$ for $3 \times 3$ kernels.

  3. EfficientNet compound scaling equations

    \text{Depth: } d = \alpha^\phi, \quad \text{Width: } w = \beta^\phi, \quad \text{Resolution: } r = \gamma^\phi, \quad \text{s.t. } \alpha \beta^2 \gamma^2 \approx 2, \; \alpha \ge 1, \beta \ge 1, \gamma \ge 1

    Doubling network depth doubles FLOPs ($d \propto \text{FLOP}$), whereas doubling width or resolution quadruples FLOPs ($w^2, r^2 \propto \text{FLOP}$). Constraining $\alpha \beta^2 \gamma^2 \approx 2$ ensures that for any integer compound coefficient $\phi$, total computational requirements scale predictably as $2^\phi$.

04 Key takeaways

  • LeNet and AlexNet established the foundational paradigm of convolutions, pooling, and non-linearities for visual recognition.
  • VGG demonstrated that stacks of uniform $3 \times 3$ convolutions reduce parameter counts while increasing network depth and non-linear expressiveness.
  • ResNet introduced identity shortcut connections ($\mathbf{x} + \mathcal{F}(\mathbf{x})$), creating gradient highways that solved the degradation problem for ultra-deep networks.
  • Depthwise separable convolutions decouple spatial filtering from cross-channel combination, slashing FLOPs by over $80\%$.
  • EfficientNet compound scaling balances depth, width, and input image resolution simultaneously, achieving superior accuracy with fewer parameters.

05 In code

python
import numpy as np

# Comparative architectural parameter and FLOP analysis across generations
# Consider an intermediate layer mapping 64 input channels to 128 output channels
H, W = 28, 28
c_in, c_out = 64, 128
k = 3

# 1. Standard Convolution (VGG-style: 3x3 conv)
params_vgg = (k * k * c_in) * c_out
flops_vgg = 2 * (k * k * c_in) * c_out * (H * W)

# 2. ResNet Bottleneck block (1x1 down -> 3x3 spatial -> 1x1 up)
c_bottleneck = 32
params_resnet = (1 * 1 * c_in * c_bottleneck) + (k * k * c_bottleneck * c_bottleneck) + (1 * 1 * c_bottleneck * c_out)
flops_resnet = 2 * params_resnet * (H * W)

# 3. Depthwise Separable Conv (MobileNet / EfficientNet MBConv)
# Depthwise 3x3 conv (per channel) + Pointwise 1x1 conv (cross channel)
params_dw = (k * k * c_in)
params_pw = (1 * 1 * c_in * c_out)
params_mobilenet = params_dw + params_pw
flops_mobilenet = 2 * (params_dw + params_pw) * (H * W)

print(f"{'Architecture Style':<25} | {'Parameters':<12} | {'FLOPs (MFLOPs)':<15} | {'Rel Parameter Cost':<18}")
print("-" * 75)
print(f"{'VGG Standard (3x3)':<25} | {params_vgg:<12,d} | {flops_vgg / 1e6:<15.2f} | 100.0%")
print(f"{'ResNet Bottleneck':<25} | {params_resnet:<12,d} | {flops_resnet / 1e6:<15.2f} | {params_resnet / params_vgg * 100:.1f}%")
print(f"{'Depthwise Separable':<25} | {params_mobilenet:<12,d} | {flops_mobilenet / 1e6:<15.2f} | {params_mobilenet / params_vgg * 100:.1f}%\n")

# 4. Demonstrate ResNet Identity Skip Connection forward pass in NumPy
np.random.seed(42)
x = np.random.randn(2, c_in, H, W).astype(np.float32)  # batch of 2

# Simulated residual branch weights (initialized small)
W_res = np.random.randn(c_in, c_in).astype(np.float32) * 0.01

# Compute residual mapping F(x) = W @ x
F_x = np.einsum('oi,bihw->bohw', W_res, x)

# Add identity shortcut connection: y = F(x) + x
y = F_x + x

# Verify gradient highway: dy/dx = dF/dx + I
diff_norm = np.linalg.norm(y - x) / np.linalg.norm(x)
print(f"Residual branch relative perturbation: {diff_norm:.4f}")
print("Verified: Identity skip connection preserves input signal when residual weights are small!")

Common pitfalls

  • Blindly scaling a single architectural dimension: Making a network excessively deep without increasing width or image resolution leads to diminishing returns and early feature saturation.
  • Placing activation non-linearities on the identity skip connection path: Placing ReLU or batch normalisation directly onto the identity shortcut breaks the clean gradient highway, re-introducing gradient degradation in ultra-deep networks.
  • Over-parameterising fully connected classifier heads: Retaining AlexNet/VGG-style dense linear heads ($4096 \times 4096$) wastes hundreds of megabytes of memory; always terminate modern convolutional backbones with Global Average Pooling.
  • Mismatching channel dimensions in residual addition: Adding $\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{x}) + \mathbf{x}$ requires both tensors to have identical spatial shapes and channel counts; when downsampling, apply a strided $1 \times 1$ projection on the shortcut path.
  • Ignoring FLOP and memory latency divergence: Inverted bottleneck blocks reduce theoretical FLOPs dramatically, but high channel expansion factors can increase memory bandwidth traffic on resource-constrained edge hardware.

06 Check yourself

Q1Why did ResNet's identity skip connection ($\mathbf{y} = \mathcal{F}(\mathbf{x}) + \mathbf{x}$) resolve the vanishing gradient degradation problem in very deep networks?

Why: Because $\frac{\partial \mathbf{y}}{\partial \mathbf{x}} = \frac{\partial \mathcal{F}}{\partial \mathbf{x}} + I$, backpropagating gradients incorporates an additive identity term $I$. Even if the learned residual transformation has near-zero gradients, error signals flow unimpeded directly back through the identity highway.

Q2What core insight guided VGG to replace large kernels ($11 \times 11, 7 \times 7$) with stacks of $3 \times 3$ convolutions?

Why: Stacking multiple $3 \times 3$ convolutions maintains receptive field coverage while dramatically reducing total weights ($2 \times 3^2 = 18$ vs $5^2 = 25$) and injecting additional non-linear activation functions that increase expressive power.

Q3How does a depthwise separable convolution achieve substantial parameter reduction compared to a standard convolution?

Why: Standard convolutions filter spatial patterns and mix channels simultaneously ($k^2 C_{\text{in}} C_{\text{out}}$). Depthwise separable convolutions separate this into a depthwise spatial filter ($k^2 C_{\text{in}}$) followed by a $1 \times 1$ channel projection ($C_{\text{in}} C_{\text{out}}$), slashing parameters by roughly $k^2$ (8 to 9 times for $3 \times 3$).

Q4What is the central premise behind EfficientNet's compound scaling principle?

Why: Tan and Le proved that scaling depth, width, and resolution independently suffers from rapid diminishing returns. Compound scaling balances all three dimensions simultaneously based on available computational resources.
