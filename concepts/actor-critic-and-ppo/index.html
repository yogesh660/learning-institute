Actor–Critic Architectures and Proximal Policy Optimisation (PPO)

The modern gold standard in policy gradient algorithms, combining actor-critic variance reduction with clipped surrogate objectives.

01 The idea

Policy gradient methods like REINFORCE suffer from high variance and sample inefficiency, while standard policy iteration risks catastrophic performance collapse from excessively large gradient steps. Proximal Policy Optimisation (PPO) combines actor-critic value baselines with a clipped surrogate objective that constrains policy updates within a trust region.

In basic policy gradients (REINFORCE), the policy $\pi_\theta(a|s)$ is updated using empirical episode returns $G_t$. Because a complete episode incorporates hundreds of stochastic transitions, returns have enormous variance, demanding millions of training steps. Actor-Critic architectures solve this by training two networks: the Actor (policy $\pi_\theta$) selects actions, while the Critic (value function $V_\phi(s)$) evaluates states, replacing noisy returns with a low-variance Advantage estimate $A(s, a) = r + \gamma V(s') - V(s)$.

However, taking standard gradient descent steps on policy gradients can cause the updated policy to diverge catastrophically into degenerate behaviour. TRPO solved this using complex second-order natural gradients and KL constraints. PPO achieves the same safety using a simple first-order clipped surrogate loss: if a proposed update shifts the probability ratio $r_t(\theta) = \pi_\theta(a_t|s_t) / \pi_{\theta_{\text{old}}}(a_t|s_t)$ beyond $[1 - \epsilon, 1 + \epsilon]$, the objective is clipped, eliminating destructive policy drift.

In everyday terms

Imagine an apprentice driver learning on an icy road. If the instructor lets the apprentice yank the steering wheel completely to the left whenever they hit a skid, the car spins out of control. PPO acts like a mechanical governor on the steering column: it allows the driver to adjust the steering smoothly, but physically clips extreme jerks to keep the car safely on the road.

02 Step by step

  1. Collect trajectory rollouts: execute current policy $\pi_{\theta_{\text{old}}}$ across $N$ parallel environments for $T$ timesteps.
  2. Estimate advantage values: compute Generalised Advantage Estimation (GAE) using Critic values $V_\phi(s_t)$.
  3. Compute probability ratios: evaluate $r_t(\theta) = \frac{\pi_\theta(a_t|s_t)}{\pi_{\theta_{\text{old}}}(a_t|s_t)}$ for batch transitions.
  4. Calculate clipped surrogate loss: compute $\min(r_t(\theta) \hat{A}_t, \text{clip}(r_t(\theta), 1-\epsilon, 1+\epsilon) \hat{A}_t)$.
  5. Update actor and critic: perform multiple epochs of minibatch SGD on composite loss combining clipped policy loss, critic value loss, and entropy bonus.
  6. Update reference policy: set $\theta_{\text{old}} \leftarrow \theta$ after completing optimisation epochs.

03 The maths

  1. PPO Clipped Surrogate Objective

    L^{\text{CLIP}}(\theta) = \hat{\mathbb{E}}_t \left[ \min\left( r_t(\theta) \hat{A}_t, \, \text{clip}(r_t(\theta), 1 - \epsilon, 1 + \epsilon) \hat{A}_t \right) \right]

    Clips probability ratio $r_t(\theta)$ within $[1-\epsilon, 1+\epsilon]$ (typically $\epsilon=0.2$) to prevent overly ambitious policy updates.

  2. Generalised Advantage Estimation (GAE)

    \hat{A}_t^{\text{GAE}(\gamma, \lambda)} = \sum_{l=0}^\infty (\gamma \lambda)^l \delta_{t+l}^V, \quad \delta_t^V = r_t + \gamma V(s_{t+1}) - V(s_t)

    Balances bias and variance in advantage estimation through exponential discounting factor $\lambda \in [0, 1]$.

04 Key takeaways

  • Actor-Critic separates policy control (actor) from state evaluation (critic) to reduce gradient variance.
  • PPO clips policy probability ratios to enforce conservative, monotonic policy improvements without complex matrix inversions.
  • Generalised Advantage Estimation (GAE) provides a tunable bias-variance trade-off for multi-step advantage calculations.

05 In code

python
import numpy as np

def ppo_clip_loss(pi_probs, pi_old_probs, advantages, epsilon=0.2):
    """Computes PPO clipped surrogate policy loss."""
    # Probability ratio r_t(theta)
    ratios = pi_probs / (pi_old_probs + 1e-8)
    
    # Unclipped and clipped objective terms
    surr1 = ratios * advantages
    surr2 = np.clip(ratios, 1.0 - epsilon, 1.0 + epsilon) * advantages
    
    # Pessimistic lower bound (minimise negative for gradient ascent)
    policy_loss = -np.mean(np.minimum(surr1, surr2))
    return policy_loss, ratios

# Simulation: 4 actions with estimated advantages
np.random.seed(42)
advantages = np.array([1.5, -0.8, 2.1, -1.2])

# Policy probs under old policy vs candidate updated policy
old_probs = np.array([0.25, 0.40, 0.20, 0.60])
new_probs = np.array([0.35, 0.38, 0.45, 0.40])  # Action 3 doubled in prob!

loss, ratios = ppo_clip_loss(new_probs, old_probs, advantages, epsilon=0.2)

print("PPO Clipped Objective Simulation:")
for i in range(len(advantages)):
    print(f"  Sample {i}: Ratio={ratios[i]:.2f}, Adv={advantages[i]:+.1f}")
print(f"\nSurrogate Policy Loss: {loss:.4f}")

Common pitfalls

  • Normalising advantages across the mini-batch incorrectly or omitting reward scaling, which destabilizes critic value fitting.
  • Setting the clipping parameter $\epsilon$ too large ($> 0.3$), which permits destructive policy shifts that collapse learning.

06 Check yourself

Q1What is the primary operational advantage of the PPO clipped objective over standard policy gradients?

Why: Clipping prevents the new policy from moving too far from the old policy in a single iteration, stabilizing gradient ascent.

Q2In an Actor-Critic architecture, what does the Critic predict?

Why: The Critic estimates the value function $V(s)$, which serves as a baseline to compute the advantage $A(s, a) = Q(s, a) - V(s)$.

Q3Why was PPO widely adopted over Trust Region Policy Optimisation (TRPO)?

Why: TRPO requires conjugate gradient optimisation to invert Fisher information matrices, whereas PPO achieves similar trust-region stability with simple first-order clipped objectives.
