GloVe and FastText

GloVe (Global Vectors for Word Representation) and FastText are the two dominant pre-neural and subword static embedding architectures in NLP. GloVe bridges the divide between global matrix factorisation (LSA) and local context window models (Word2Vec) by fitting log-bilinear word vectors directly to global co-occurrence probability ratios. FastText enriches word representations with character n-gram subwords, enabling robust handling of morphologically rich languages, rare vocabulary inflections, and out-of-vocabulary (OOV) tokens.

01 The idea

Word2Vec revolutionized NLP by training shallow neural networks on sliding windows, but it suffered from two key limitations: it discarded global corpus statistics by processing individual context windows greedily, and it treated words as atomic, indivisible symbols. If a test document contained a slight spelling variation or rare morphological compound like "unquestionably", Word2Vec threw up its hands and returned an unknown token. GloVe solved the global statistical aggregation problem; FastText solved the subword morphology and out-of-vocabulary dilemma.

Ratios of co-occurrence probabilities encode relational meaning: Pennington, Socher, and Manning observed that semantic meaning is best captured not by raw co-occurrence counts, but by ratios of co-occurrence probabilities. Consider the target words "ice" and "steam" probed against context word k: for k = "solid", P(solid | ice) / P(solid | steam) is large; for k = "gas", the ratio is tiny; for k = "water" or k = "fashion", the ratio is near 1. Vector differences (w_ice - w_steam) dot-producted with w_k naturally model this log-probability ratio.

Weighted least squares and the dampening function: In a massive text corpus, co-occurrence counts range from 1 to millions. If all co-occurrences were weighted equally, frequent stop words ("the", "of") would dominate the loss function, while rare pairs would drown in noise. GloVe introduces a soft weighting function f(X_ij) = min(1, (X_ij / x_max)^0.75) that prevents stop words from dominating while avoiding over-weighting rare occurrences.

FastText and the power of subword morphology: In languages like German ("Donaudampfschiffahrtsgesellschaftskapitän"), Turkish, or Russian, words are built from rich agglutinative morphemes. FastText represents each word as the sum of its character n-grams bounded by special boundary symbols "<" and ">" (e.g. for "where" with n = 3: ""). When an unknown word is encountered at inference time, FastText simply sums the representations of its constituent subwords, yielding an accurate semantic embedding out of the box.

In everyday terms

Think of learning vocabulary like cooking. Word2Vec and GloVe treat every dish as an indivisible monolithic mystery: "beef stew" is a completely distinct entity from "lamb stew" or "stewed carrots". If someone orders "elk stew", the restaurant says "I have never heard of that dish; I cannot cook it" (out of vocabulary). FastText looks inside the recipe at the elemental ingredients: it knows what "elk" means as meat, and it knows the cooking technique of "stew". By assembling the subword ingredients, it can prepare and understand "elk stew" flawlessly even if it has never appeared on the menu before.

03 The maths

  1. GloVe log-bilinear objective function

    J = \sum_{i=1}^V \sum_{j=1}^V f(X_{i, j}) \left( \mathbf{w}_i^T \tilde{\mathbf{w}}_j + b_i + \tilde{b}_j - \ln X_{i, j} \right)^2

    Here $\mathbf{w}_i, \tilde{\mathbf{w}}_j \in \mathbb{R}^d$ are word and context vectors, $b_i, \tilde{b}_j$ are scalar biases, and $X_{i, j}$ is the co-occurrence count between word $i$ and word $j$. The model fits inner products directly to the logarithm of co-occurrence probabilities.

  2. GloVe co-occurrence weighting function

    f(x) = \begin{cases} \left( \frac{x}{x_{\max}} \right)^\alpha & \text{if } x < x_{\max} \\ 1 & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}

    Hyperparameters are typically set to $\alpha = 0.75$ and $x_{\max} = 100$. The fractional power dampens high-frequency co-occurrences while ensuring non-zero gradients on rare co-occurrences.

  3. FastText subword vector composition

    \mathbf{v}_w = \sum_{g \in \mathcal{G}_w} \mathbf{z}_g

    Here $\mathcal{G}_w$ is the set of character $n$-grams present in word $w$ (including the whole word symbol $\langle w \rangle$), and $\mathbf{z}_g \in \mathbb{R}^d$ is the learned embedding for subword $g$. This additive formulation naturally produces embeddings for novel out-of-vocabulary words.

04 Key takeaways

  • GloVe fits a log-bilinear model to global co-occurrence probability ratios, uniting global matrix factorisation with local window methods.
  • GloVe uses a sublinear weighting function f(X_ij) = (X_ij / x_max)^0.75 to prevent ubiquitous stop words from dominating parameter updates.
  • Final GloVe embeddings are formed by averaging or summing the target vectors w and context vectors w_tilde.
  • FastText enriches token representations by decomposing words into character n-grams bounded by '<' and '>'.
  • FastText achieves zero Out-of-Vocabulary (OOV) rates by composing unseen words from their known constituent subword embeddings.

05 In code

python
import math
from collections import defaultdict, Counter

class FastTextToy:
    """
    Subword character n-gram extraction and out-of-vocabulary
    embedding composition demonstration.
    """
    def __init__(self, min_n=3, max_n=5, dim=8):
        self.min_n = min_n
        self.max_n = max_n
        self.dim = dim
        self.subword_embeddings = {}
        
    def _get_ngrams(self, word):
        """Extract character n-grams bounded by < and >."""
        padded = f"<{word}>"
        ngrams = [padded]  # Include whole word
        L = len(padded)
        for n in range(self.min_n, min(self.max_n + 1, L)):
            for i in range(L - n + 1):
                ngrams.append(padded[i:i+n])
        return ngrams

    def train_mock(self, word_vector_dict):
        """Simulate learned subword embeddings from known vocabulary."""
        for word, target_vec in word_vector_dict.items():
            ngrams = self._get_ngrams(word)
            # Distribute mock vector across subwords
            for g in ngrams:
                if g not in self.subword_embeddings:
                    # Deterministic hash-based mock initialization
                    h = hash(g) % 1000
                    self.subword_embeddings[g] = [
                        math.sin(h + i) * 0.1 for i in range(self.dim)
                    ]

    def get_embedding(self, word):
        """Compose embedding by summing constituent subword vectors."""
        ngrams = self._get_ngrams(word)
        vec = [0.0] * self.dim
        found = 0
        for g in ngrams:
            if g in self.subword_embeddings:
                for i in range(self.dim):
                    vec[i] += self.subword_embeddings[g][i]
                found += 1
                
        # Average subwords if found
        if found > 0:
            vec = [v / found for v in vec]
        return vec

def cosine_sim(v1, v2):
    dot = sum(a * b for a, b in zip(v1, v2))
    norm1 = math.sqrt(sum(a * a for a in v1))
    norm2 = math.sqrt(sum(b * b for b in v2))
    return dot / (norm1 * norm2) if norm1 > 0 and norm2 > 0 else 0.0

# Initialize FastText toy model
ft = FastTextToy(min_n=3, max_n=4, dim=8)

# Known training vocabulary
known_words = {
    "play": [1.0] * 8,
    "player": [1.0] * 8,
    "playing": [1.0] * 8,
    "playful": [1.0] * 8,
    "work": [-1.0] * 8,
    "worker": [-1.0] * 8,
    "working": [-1.0] * 8
}
ft.train_mock(known_words)

# Out-of-vocabulary word (never seen during training)
oov_word = "playfully"
known_subword_count = len([g for g in ft._get_ngrams(oov_word) if g in ft.subword_embeddings])

print(f"Known training words: {list(known_words.keys())}")
print(f"Testing OOV word: '{oov_word}'")
print(f"Subwords in '{oov_word}': {ft._get_ngrams(oov_word)}")
print(f"Matched known subwords: {known_subword_count} / {len(ft._get_ngrams(oov_word))}")

v_oov = ft.get_embedding(oov_word)
v_play = ft.get_embedding("playful")
v_work = ft.get_embedding("worker")

print(f"\nCosine similarity between OOV '{oov_word}' and 'playful': {cosine_sim(v_oov, v_play):+.4f}")
print(f"Cosine similarity between OOV '{oov_word}' and 'worker':  {cosine_sim(v_oov, v_work):+.4f}")

Common pitfalls

  • Using GloVe without sufficient corpus scale: GloVe relies on dense, stable global co-occurrence statistics; training on small corpora (< 100 million tokens) produces noisy, unaligned embeddings. Use pretrained 6B, 42B, or 840B GloVe weights for small datasets.
  • Hash collisions in FastText subword tables: FastText maps millions of possible character n-grams into a fixed-size embedding table (typically 2 million buckets) via hashing. On very small tables, high collision rates degrade rare word representations.
  • Character n-gram range selection: Using n-grams that are too short (n = 2) captures noisy character pairs; using n-grams that are too long (n > 6) fails to generalise across morphological stems. Standard practice uses n in [3, 6].
  • Discarding whole-word tokens in FastText: FastText explicitly retains the full padded token '' alongside its subwords. Discarding the full-word token prevents the model from capturing idiosyncratic non-compositional idioms.

06 Check yourself

Q1What core mathematical insight motivated the design of the GloVe objective function?

Why: Pennington et al. showed that the ratio P(k|w_i) / P(k|w_j) captures subtle semantic distinctions between words i and j. In log-space, this ratio corresponds to the difference between dot products (w_i - w_j)^T w_k, leading directly to the GloVe objective.

Q2Why does FastText outperform standard Word2Vec and GloVe on morphologically rich languages like German, Turkish, and Finnish?

Why: Word2Vec and GloVe assign one monolithic vector per word token. In agglutinative languages with millions of inflected verb and compound noun forms, words appear rarely in text. FastText shares embeddings across character n-grams, allowing morphological variants to share parameters.

Q3What is the purpose of the weighting function f(X_ij) = (X_ij / x_max)^alpha in GloVe?

Why: Without weighting, extremely common pairs like ("the", "of") would account for the majority of the total squared error, causing the model to optimise solely for stop words. The sublinear curve (alpha = 0.75, capped at x_max) balances frequent and infrequent pairs.
