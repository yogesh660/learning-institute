01 The idea

Traditional clustering groups rows across all features or features across all samples. Biclustering identifies local sub-matrices where a specific subset of samples exhibits coherent behaviour across a specific subset of features.

Traditional clustering algorithms partition observations based on their values across all measured features: k-means or DBSCAN compares samples along the entirety of feature space $\mathbb{R}^d$. However, in genomics, e-commerce, and text mining, relationships are often strictly localized: a group of 50 genes may be co-regulated only in a tiny subset of 12 cancer patients, while expressing unrelated random baseline noise across the other 500 patients.

When classical clustering evaluates distance over all 20,000 genes, the localized 50-gene co-regulation is completely overwhelmed by the background noise of the other 19,950 uninformative genes. Similarly, in collaborative filtering, a group of 300 users may share identical preferences for French arthouse cinema, but have completely divergent preferences across Hollywood blockbusters and sports documentaries.

Biclustering (also termed co-clustering or two-mode clustering) solves this by clustering rows and columns simultaneously. Rather than partitioning the entire row space or column space globally, biclustering identifies local sub-matrices (biclusters) where a specific subset of rows exhibits coherent, correlated, or constant values across a specific subset of columns.

Spectral Co-clustering models the data matrix as an adjacency matrix of an undirected bipartite graph between row and column nodes. By computing singular vectors of the normalised data matrix, it solves a graph partition problem that rearranges rows and columns to expose block-diagonal and checkerboard structures.

In everyday terms

Imagine a giant crossword puzzle grid filled with random jumbled letters. If you look at the whole grid at once, it appears completely chaotic. Biclustering is like finding hidden word rectangles: taking five specific rows and four specific columns, then sliding them together so their intersection forms a perfectly readable English word, even while all surrounding letters remain gibberish.

03 The maths

  1. Mean Squared Residue (Cheng and Church Score)

    H(I, J) = \frac{1}{|I| |J|} \sum_{i \in I, j \in J} (a_{ij} - a_{iJ} - a_{Ij} + a_{IJ})^2

    Measures coherence of a bicluster sub-matrix formed by rows I and columns J. Here a_iJ is row mean, a_Ij is column mean, and a_IJ is overall bicluster mean. A residue of zero indicates perfect additive coherence.

  2. Normalised Bipartite Graph Laplacian

    A_n = R^{-1/2} A C^{-1/2}, \quad R = \operatorname{diag}(A \mathbf{1}_p), \; C = \operatorname{diag}(\mathbf{1}_n^T A)

    Normalisation ensures degree-invariance on bipartite graph connecting row nodes to column nodes, mapping the problem directly into spectral graph theory.

  3. Spectral Co-clustering Partition

    z = \begin{bmatrix} R^{-1/2} u \\ C^{-1/2} v \end{bmatrix}, \quad A_n v = \sigma u, \; A_n^T u = \sigma v

    The left and right singular vectors u and v of normalised matrix A_n define coordinates that minimise normalised cut on the bipartite graph.

04 Key takeaways

  • Simultaneously clusters rows and columns of a matrix, isolating local sub-matrices of coherent values.
  • Vital when target patterns exist only across a subset of features for a subset of samples (e.g. gene pathways or niche product tastes).
  • Spectral Co-clustering frames the task as finding a minimal normalised cut on a bipartite graph between rows and columns.
  • Solved efficiently via Singular Value Decomposition (SVD) of the row- and column-normalised data matrix.
  • Cheng-Church score (Mean Squared Residue) provides a standard quality benchmark for additive and constant biclusters.

05 In code

python
import numpy as np
from sklearn.cluster import KMeans

# Spectral Co-Clustering from scratch
def spectral_coclustering(A, n_clusters=2):
    n_rows, n_cols = A.shape
    
    # 1. Row and Column marginals
    R = np.sum(A, axis=1, keepdims=True)
    C = np.sum(A, axis=0, keepdims=True)
    
    # Prevent division by zero
    R[R == 0] = 1e-12
    C[C == 0] = 1e-12
    
    # 2. Normalised matrix A_n = R^{-1/2} A C^{-1/2}
    R_inv_sqrt = 1.0 / np.sqrt(R)
    C_inv_sqrt = 1.0 / np.sqrt(C)
    A_n = R_inv_sqrt * A * C_inv_sqrt
    
    # 3. SVD of normalised matrix
    U, S, Vt = np.linalg.svd(A_n, full_matrices=False)
    V = Vt.T
    
    # Use singular vectors corresponding to second largest singular values
    # For n_clusters, take vectors 1 through n_clusters
    n_vecs = int(np.ceil(np.log2(n_clusters))) + 1
    U_sub = U[:, 1:n_vecs]
    V_sub = V[:, 1:n_vecs]
    
    # Scale back by R^{-1/2} and C^{-1/2}
    Z_rows = R_inv_sqrt * U_sub
    Z_cols = C_inv_sqrt * V_sub
    
    # 4. Cluster rows and columns using k-means
    km_rows = KMeans(n_clusters=n_clusters, random_state=42, n_init=10).fit(Z_rows)
    km_cols = KMeans(n_clusters=n_clusters, random_state=42, n_init=10).fit(Z_cols)
    
    return km_rows.labels_, km_cols.labels_

# Create synthetic matrix with 2 hidden block-diagonal biclusters
np.random.seed(42)
n_r, n_c = 60, 40
data = np.random.uniform(1.0, 5.0, (n_r, n_c))

# Bicluster 1: rows 0-24, cols 0-14 have elevated mean
data[0:25, 0:15] += 20.0
# Bicluster 2: rows 25-59, cols 15-39 have elevated mean
data[25:60, 15:40] += 20.0

# Shuffle rows and columns to scramble visual structure
row_perm = np.random.permutation(n_r)
col_perm = np.random.permutation(n_c)
scrambled = data[row_perm, :][:, col_perm]

# Run Spectral Co-clustering
row_labels, col_labels = spectral_coclustering(scrambled, n_clusters=2)

print(f"Scrambled matrix shape: {scrambled.shape}")
print(f"Discovered row cluster sizes: {np.bincount(row_labels)}")
print(f"Discovered column cluster sizes: {np.bincount(col_labels)}")
print("Row partition matches ground truth:", len(np.unique(row_labels[row_perm < 25])) == 1)
print("Column partition matches ground truth:", len(np.unique(col_labels[col_perm < 15])) == 1)

Common pitfalls

  • Sensitivity to negative values: Spectral co-clustering interprets elements as bipartite edge weights, requiring non-negative entries ($A_{ij} \ge 0$).
  • Overlapping biclusters: Standard spectral co-clustering produces disjoint partitions; soft or overlapping biclustering requires Plaid or iterative residue models.
  • Sensitivity to background noise scale: When matrix sparsity is extreme or noise variance exceeds signal jump, singular vectors fail to separate.

06 Check yourself

Q1Why does traditional k-means clustering fail to detect localized gene expression pathways across patients?

Why: Traditional clustering computes Euclidean distances globally across all features. When a pathway is active only in a tiny fraction of genes, global distance is dominated by irrelevant background genes.

Q2How does Spectral Co-clustering simultaneously assign cluster labels to both rows and columns?

Why: Spectral Co-clustering frames rows and columns as bipartite graph nodes. SVD on the normalised matrix yields singular vectors corresponding to graph cuts for both rows and columns.
