BERT and masked language models

BERT (Bidirectional Encoder Representations from Transformers) transformed natural language processing by enabling deep bidirectional pre-training from unlabelled text. Prior language models were constrained to left-to-right or right-to-left causal masking to prevent tokens from indirectly seeing themselves in deep multi-layer networks. BERT introduced Masked Language Modelling (MLM)—masking 15% of input tokens at random and training the Transformer encoder to predict the original words based simultaneously on left and right context. Fine-tuning a single pre-trained BERT model with minimal task-specific layers established state-of-the-art results across eleven diverse NLP benchmarks.

01 The idea

In the sentence "The bank can guarantee the deposit because of the river bank's stability", the word "bank" appears twice with completely different meanings. A left-to-right language model processing the first "bank" has only seen "The"; it cannot know whether financial or geographical concepts follow. BERT solves this fundamental bottleneck: by masking words and training with deep bidirectional self-attention across the entire sequence, every token embedding incorporates rich semantic signals from both its past and its future.

The dilemma of deep bidirectional representations: Standard language models cannot use bidirectional self-attention because allowing tokens to look left and right in multi-layer networks leads to cyclic cheating: token i in layer 2 could attend to token i+1 in layer 1, which has already attended to token i in layer 0, trivialising next-token prediction. BERT breaks this cycle with the Cloze task (Masked LM): by corrupting a small fraction of input words with [MASK], the model must use the uncorrupted surrounding context to reconstruct the missing information.

The 80/10/10 masking schedule: If BERT only encountered the artificial [MASK] token during pre-training, fine-tuning would suffer from distribution shift because real-world downstream tasks never contain [MASK] tokens. BERT mitigates this with an ingenious 80/10/10 schedule for the 15% selected tokens: 80% are replaced with [MASK], 10% are replaced with a random vocabulary token (injecting noise and forcing the model to verify every token), and 10% are left unchanged (biasing representations towards actual observed words).

Pre-train once, fine-tune everywhere: Before BERT, transfer learning in NLP was limited to static word embeddings (Word2Vec, GloVe) or feature-based LSTM representations (ELMo). BERT introduced the modern pre-train and fine-tune paradigm: pre-train a massive 110M (Base) or 340M (Large) parameter encoder on hundreds of millions of words of unlabelled text once, and then fine-tune all parameters end-to-end on task-specific labelled datasets with a single classification head in just a few epochs.

In everyday terms

Think of learning a foreign language by completing crossword puzzles or fill-in-the-blank exam questions. If you read a book from left to right, you can only guess what comes next based on what you have already read. But on a crossword puzzle, a word like "b _ _ k" has both preceding clues ("deposited funds in the") and trailing clues ("to earn compound interest"). By looking at both sides of the sentence simultaneously, your brain effortlessly resolves the blank as "bank". BERT trains a neural network on billions of fill-in-the-blank puzzles until it acquires an intuitive mastery of human grammar, idiom, and semantics.

03 The maths

  1. Masked Language Model (MLM) objective function

    \mathcal{L}_{\text{MLM}}(\theta) = -\sum_{i \in \mathcal{M}} \log P(w_i \mid \tilde{W}; \theta)

    Here $\mathcal{M}$ is the subset of randomly selected masked positions (15% of all tokens), $\tilde{W}$ is the corrupted input sequence, and $w_i$ is the true original token. Cross-entropy loss is computed exclusively on masked positions, avoiding loss computation on unmasked tokens.

  2. Prediction head token probability distribution

    P(w \mid \tilde{W}; \theta) = \operatorname{softmax}\left( \mathbf{W}_v \operatorname{GELU}(\mathbf{W}_h \mathbf{h}_i + \mathbf{b}_h) + \mathbf{b}_v \right)_w

    Here $\mathbf{h}_i \in \mathbb{R}^d$ is the final encoder output vector at masked position $i$, $\mathbf{W}_h \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times d}$, and $\mathbf{W}_v \in \mathbb{R}^{|V| \times d}$ is the vocabulary projection matrix (often tied with input token embeddings to conserve parameters).

  3. Next Sentence Prediction (NSP) binary objective

    \mathcal{L}_{\text{NSP}}(\theta) = -\log P(\text{IsNext} \in \{0, 1\} \mid \mathbf{h}_{[\text{CLS}]}; \theta)

    In original BERT, a binary classification loss was applied to the special pooled $[\text{CLS}]$ token to predict whether sentence B naturally followed sentence A (50% actual continuation, 50% random sentence). Later architectures (RoBERTa) proved NSP was unnecessary when training on contiguous text blocks.

04 Key takeaways

  • BERT uses bidirectional self-attention to encode left and right linguistic context simultaneously across all layers.
  • Masked Language Modelling (MLM) corrupts 15% of tokens, training the encoder on a fill-in-the-blank Cloze task without trivial autoregressive leakage.
  • The 80/10/10 masking rule prevents distribution mismatch between pre-training and downstream inference where [MASK] tokens never appear.
  • Input representations combine WordPiece token embeddings, segment embeddings (sentence A vs B), and learned absolute position embeddings.
  • BERT established the pre-train and fine-tune paradigm: pre-training on unlabelled text produces representations that specialise to downstream tasks in just a few epochs.

05 In code

python
import numpy as np
import random

class MLMMasker:
    """
    Simulation of BERT 80/10/10 Masked Language Modeling (MLM)
    corruption schedule on tokenised sequences.
    """
    def __init__(self, vocab, mask_prob=0.15):
        self.vocab = vocab
        self.mask_prob = mask_prob
        self.mask_token = "[MASK]"
        
    def mask_sequence(self, tokens):
        """
        Apply BERT 80/10/10 masking rule to tokens.
        Returns corrupted tokens, masked indices, and ground truth labels.
        """
        corrupted = list(tokens)
        masked_indices = []
        labels = []
        
        for i, token in enumerate(tokens):
            # Skip special boundary tokens
            if token in ["[CLS]", "[SEP]"]:
                continue
                
            if random.random() < self.mask_prob:
                masked_indices.append(i)
                labels.append(token)
                
                rand_val = random.random()
                if rand_val < 0.80:
                    # 80% of time: Replace with [MASK]
                    corrupted[i] = self.mask_token
                elif rand_val < 0.90:
                    # 10% of time: Replace with random vocabulary word
                    corrupted[i] = random.choice(self.vocab)
                else:
                    # 10% of time: Keep unchanged
                    corrupted[i] = token
                    
        return corrupted, masked_indices, labels

def softmax(x):
    e_x = np.exp(x - np.max(x, axis=-1, keepdims=True))
    return e_x / np.sum(e_x, axis=-1, keepdims=True)

# Vocabulary and sample sentence
vocab = ["the", "quick", "brown", "fox", "jumps", "over", "lazy", "dog", "bank", "river", "capital"]
tokens = ["[CLS]", "the", "brown", "fox", "jumps", "over", "the", "river", "bank", "[SEP]"]

random.seed(42)
masker = MLMMasker(vocab, mask_prob=0.30)  # Elevated probability for demonstration
corrupted_tokens, masked_idx, gold_labels = masker.mask_sequence(tokens)

print("Original Tokens:")
print(" ", " ".join(tokens))
print("\nCorrupted Tokens (after 80/10/10 masking):")
print(" ", " ".join(corrupted_tokens))
print("\nMasked Positions & Targets:")
for idx, target in zip(masked_idx, gold_labels):
    print(f"  Pos {idx}: observed '{corrupted_tokens[idx]}' -> Target: '{target}'")

# Simulate MLM prediction head logits over vocabulary
V = len(vocab)
mock_logits = np.random.randn(len(masked_idx), V)
mock_probs = softmax(mock_logits)

# Compute Cross-Entropy Loss over masked positions
loss = 0.0
for i, target in enumerate(gold_labels):
    target_idx = vocab.index(target) if target in vocab else 0
    prob = mock_probs[i, target_idx]
    loss -= np.log(max(prob, 1e-9))
loss /= len(masked_idx)

print(f"\nSimulated Cross-Entropy MLM Loss across {len(masked_idx)} positions: {loss:.4f}")

Common pitfalls

  • Pre-training vs inference mismatch: If you only replace masked words with [MASK], the model learns that representations only need updating when [MASK] is visible, degrading performance on downstream clean text. Always adhere to the 80/10/10 corruption schedule.
  • Using BERT for autoregressive text generation: BERT is an encoder designed for representation learning and classification, not generation. Generating text by iteratively masking and predicting is orders of magnitude slower and less coherent than decoder-only models (like GPT).
  • Exceeding the 512-token maximum sequence length: Standard BERT uses fixed absolute positional embeddings capped at 512 tokens. Longer documents must be chunked or processed with sliding window strategies.
  • Catastrophic forgetting during fine-tuning: Fine-tuning with learning rates that are too high (> 1e-4) destroys pre-trained representations in the lower layers. Use gentle learning rates (2e-5 to 5e-5) with linear warm-up.

06 Check yourself

Q1Why did BERT introduce the 80/10/10 masking rule instead of replacing all 15% masked words with the [MASK] token?

Why: If BERT only trained on [MASK] tokens, the network would never learn to contextualise clean, uncorrupted words at test time. Replacing 10% with random words forces the model to maintain contextual representations for every token, and keeping 10% unchanged biases embeddings towards actual observed words.

Q2What makes bidirectional self-attention in BERT fundamentally different from the causal attention used in decoder-only models like GPT?

Why: In causal attention, an upper-triangular mask of negative infinities forces token i to attend only to tokens j <= i. BERT does not apply a causal mask, allowing full bidirectional attention across all positions.

Q3How does BERT format sentence pairs (such as question-answering or premise-hypothesis pairs) for fine-tuning?

Why: BERT concatenates both sentences into a single continuous sequence with segment embeddings E_A and E_B. This enables full cross-attention between every token in Sentence A and Sentence B across all Transformer layers.
