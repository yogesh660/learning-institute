ROC Curves and AUC

The ROC curve plots true-positive rate against false-positive rate at every threshold. AUC is the chance a random positive outranks a random negative.

01 The idea

Sweep the threshold from high to low and plot recall (TPR) against FPR. A perfect model hugs the top-left corner; a random one follows the diagonal. The area under the curve summarises ranking quality in one number.

AUC has a lovely interpretation: pick one positive and one negative at random; AUC is the probability the model scores the positive higher. It does not depend on any threshold or on the class balance, which is both its strength (ranking quality) and its weakness (it can look great on highly imbalanced problems where precision is poor).

In everyday terms

A sorting test: line everyone up by the model’s score and check how often the people who should be ahead actually are.

02 Step by step

  1. Score held-out data.
  2. For every threshold compute TPR and FPR.
  3. Plot the curve; integrate it for AUC (or compute the rank statistic directly).
  4. Pick an operating point from the curve, given your FPR budget.

03 Try it

Control how far apart the two classes’ scores are. The ROC curve and its AUC update live; your threshold is the marked point.

Things to try
  1. Set separation to 0: the curve collapses to the diagonal, AUC ≈ 0.5.
  2. Push separation up and watch the curve bend toward the corner.

04 The maths

  1. AUC as a probability

    $$\mathrm{AUC} = P\big(s(x^+) > s(x^-)\big) = \frac{1}{n_+n_-}\sum_{i \in +}\sum_{j \in -}\mathbb 1[s_i > s_j]$$

    Equivalent to the Mann–Whitney U statistic, scaled.

05 Key takeaways

  • AUC 0.5 = random; 1.0 = perfect ranking.
  • AUC ignores calibration: multiplying all scores by 2 does not change it.
  • On rare positives, also report precision–recall.

06 In code

python
from sklearn.metrics import roc_curve, roc_auc_score
proba = model.predict_proba(X_te)[:, 1]
fpr, tpr, thr = roc_curve(y_te, proba)
print("AUC:", round(roc_auc_score(y_te, proba), 3))
print("TPR at 5% FPR:", tpr[fpr <= 0.05].max().round(3))

Common pitfalls

  • Treating a high AUC as proof the model is useful at the threshold you deploy.
  • Comparing AUCs computed on different test sets.
