Total variation, Hellinger and Bhattacharyya distances

Total Variation distance, Hellinger distance, and Bhattacharyya distance form the foundational geometric family of probability metrics grounded in statistical distinguishability and f-divergences. While Total Variation quantifies the maximum possible difference in probability assigned to any measurable event, Hellinger distance provides a true metric on the unit Hilbert sphere via square-root density mappings, obeying the triangle inequality and establishing tight bounds on minimax estimation error via Le Cam's inequalities. The Bhattacharyya distance connects these metrics to optimal Bayes classification error bounds.

01 The idea

How different are two probability distributions, and what is the absolute worst-case error you could make if you used model Q instead of true distribution P? While KL divergence is asymmetric, unbounded, and violates the triangle inequality, Total Variation distance is bounded in [0, 1] and directly measures the maximum discrepancy across all possible events. Hellinger distance turns probability densities into Euclidean vectors on a sphere. Mastering these classical metrics provides the theoretical foundation for differential privacy, Markov chain convergence, and statistical minimax theory.

Total Variation as Maximum Disagreement: Suppose an auditor inspects two probability models P and Q. The auditor is allowed to pick any conceivable event A (e.g. "tomorrow's temperature exceeds 30C"). The Total Variation distance D_TV(P, Q) is the maximum possible difference |P(A) - Q(A)| the auditor could ever find. If D_TV = 0.05, the two models will never differ by more than 5 percentage points on any forecast.

Hellinger Distance as Sphere Geometry: Probability densities integrate to 1: int p(x) dx = 1. If we take the square root sqrt(p(x)), then int (sqrt(p(x)))^2 dx = 1. This means sqrt(p(x)) is a unit vector on an infinite-dimensional hypersphere! The Hellinger distance is simply the standard Euclidean distance between these two square-root unit vectors, scaled by 1/sqrt(2). Because it is an actual metric space distance, it satisfies symmetry and the triangle inequality!

The Bhattacharyya Affinity: The inner product between the two square-root vectors is the Bhattacharyya coefficient rho(P, Q) = int sqrt(p(x) q(x)) dx in [0, 1]. If the distributions are identical, rho = 1; if they have disjoint supports, rho = 0. The Bhattacharyya distance -log rho provides an upper bound on Bayes classification error.

Le Cam's Inequalities and Minimax Rates: In statistical decision theory, Le Cam's lemmas prove that the ability of any algorithm to distinguish between two hypothetical data distributions is fundamentally bounded by their Total Variation and Hellinger distances: H^2(P, Q) <= D_TV(P, Q) <= sqrt(2) * H(P, Q).

In everyday terms

Think of two light beams shining through stained glass. Total Variation is the maximum difference in brightness you could see through any filter. Hellinger distance treats each light beam as an arrow pointing to the surface of a globe, measuring the straight-line physical distance between the two arrow tips through 3D space.

03 The maths

  1. Total Variation Distance Formulation

    D_{\text{TV}}(P, Q) = \sup_{A \in \mathcal{F}} \big| P(A) - Q(A) \big| = \frac{1}{2} \int_{\mathcal{X}} \big| p(x) - q(x) \big| \, dx = \frac{1}{2} \|p - q\|_1

    Total Variation is the supremum difference over all measurable events A. It equals half the L_1 distance between the densities, bounded in [0, 1], where 0 indicates identical distributions and 1 indicates mutually singular distributions with disjoint supports.

  2. Squared Hellinger Distance and Bhattacharyya Coefficient

    H^2(P, Q) = \frac{1}{2} \int_{\mathcal{X}} \left( \sqrt{p(x)} - \sqrt{q(x)} \right)^2 dx = 1 - \int_{\mathcal{X}} \sqrt{p(x) q(x)} \, dx = 1 - \rho(P, Q)

    The Hellinger distance is a true metric satisfying the triangle inequality. The quantity \rho(P, Q) = \int \sqrt{p(x) q(x)} \, dx is the Bhattacharyya affinity coefficient. For product distributions P = \prod P_i and Q = \prod Q_i, the affinity factorises multiplicatively: \rho(P, Q) = \prod \rho(P_i, Q_i).

  3. Le Cam's Sandwich Inequalities

    H^2(P, Q) \le D_{\text{TV}}(P, Q) \le H(P, Q) \sqrt{2 - H^2(P, Q)} \le \sqrt{2} H(P, Q)

    Le Cam's inequalities connect Total Variation and Hellinger distance. Because Hellinger distance factorises cleanly across independent observations, it is widely used to prove minimax lower bounds in statistical estimation.

04 Key takeaways

  • Total Variation distance quantifies the maximum possible difference in probability assigned to any measurable event, bounded in [0, 1].
  • Hellinger distance is a true metric obeying the triangle inequality, measuring Euclidean distance between square-root density vectors on the unit Hilbert sphere.
  • The Bhattacharyya distance directly establishes an upper bound on the minimum Bayes classification error rate.
  • Le Cam's inequalities tightly sandwich Total Variation between H^2 and sqrt(2)*H, enabling minimax sample complexity proofs.

05 In code

python
"""
Total Variation, Hellinger, and Bhattacharyya Distances
Calculates exact distances for Gaussian distributions and verifies
Le Cam's bounding inequalities.
"""

import numpy as np

def compute_distances_gaussian(mu1: float, sig1: float, mu2: float, sig2: float):
    """
    Compute analytical Bhattacharyya, Hellinger, and numerical Total Variation
    between two univariate Gaussians N(mu1, sig1^2) and N(mu2, sig2^2).
    """
    v1, v2 = sig1**2, sig2**2
    
    # 1. Bhattacharyya Distance for 1D Gaussians
    # D_B = (1/4) * ln( (1/4)*(v1/v2 + v2/v1 + 2) ) + (1/4) * ( (mu1 - mu2)^2 / (v1 + v2) )
    term1 = 0.25 * np.log(0.25 * (v1/v2 + v2/v1 + 2.0))
    term2 = 0.25 * ((mu1 - mu2)**2 / (v1 + v2))
    d_bhattacharyya = term1 + term2
    
    # Bhattacharyya affinity rho = exp(-D_B)
    rho = np.exp(-d_bhattacharyya)
    
    # 2. Hellinger Distance: H^2 = 1 - rho
    h_squared = 1.0 - rho
    h_distance = np.sqrt(h_squared)
    
    # 3. Total Variation Distance via numerical integration
    x_grid = np.linspace(min(mu1 - 4*sig1, mu2 - 4*sig2), max(mu1 + 4*sig1, mu2 + 4*sig2), 20000)
    dx = x_grid[1] - x_grid[0]
    p_dens = (1.0 / (np.sqrt(2 * np.pi) * sig1)) * np.exp(-0.5 * ((x_grid - mu1) / sig1)**2)
    q_dens = (1.0 / (np.sqrt(2 * np.pi) * sig2)) * np.exp(-0.5 * ((x_grid - mu2) / sig2)**2)
    tv_distance = 0.5 * np.sum(np.abs(p_dens - q_dens)) * dx
    
    return {
        "bhattacharyya_dist": round(d_bhattacharyya, 4),
        "affinity_rho": round(rho, 4),
        "hellinger_squared": round(h_squared, 4),
        "hellinger_dist": round(h_distance, 4),
        "total_variation": round(tv_distance, 4),
    }

# Experiment: P ~ N(0, 1), Q ~ N(mu, 1) as mu shifts
print("=== Metric Comparison for N(0, 1) vs N(mu, 1) ===")
for mu in [0.0, 0.5, 1.0, 2.0, 3.5]:
    m = compute_distances_gaussian(0.0, 1.0, mu, 1.0)
    h2 = m["hellinger_squared"]
    tv = m["total_variation"]
    upper_bound = np.sqrt(2 * h2)
    print(f"Shift mu = {mu:3.1f} | TV: {tv:6.4f} | H^2: {h2:6.4f} | Bhattacharyya: {m['bhattacharyya_dist']:6.4f}")
    print(f"    Le Cam Bounds Check: H^2 ({h2:.4f}) <= TV ({tv:.4f}) <= sqrt(2)*H ({upper_bound:.4f}) -> {h2 <= tv <= upper_bound + 1e-4}")

Common pitfalls

  • Confusing Total Variation with total variance: Total Variation is a bounded probability divergence, whereas variance measures second-order dispersion around the mean.
  • Assuming Bhattacharyya distance satisfies the triangle inequality: The Bhattacharyya distance -ln(\rho) is not a true metric (it violates the triangle inequality); only Hellinger distance is a true metric.
  • Overlooking product factorisation: For multi-dimensional independent variables, the Bhattacharyya affinity \rho factorises as a simple product, making Hellinger distance vastly easier to compute than Total Variation.

06 Check yourself

Q1What is the maximum possible value of the Total Variation distance between two probability distributions?

Why: Total Variation is defined as the supremum difference in event probabilities |P(A) - Q(A)|. Since probabilities are bounded in [0, 1], D_TV is strictly bounded in [0, 1].

Q2Why is Hellinger distance preferred over KL divergence in statistical minimax lower bound proofs?

Why: KL divergence is asymmetric and violates the triangle inequality. Hellinger distance is a genuine metric on the unit sphere whose affinity factorises multiplicatively across independent dimensions, facilitating Le Cam's minimax bounds.

Q3How does the Bhattacharyya distance D_B relate to the minimum achievable Bayes classification error rate?

Why: The Bhattacharyya bound is a celebrated result in statistical pattern recognition: the probability of misclassification under the optimal Bayes classifier is bounded above by (1/2) * exp(-D_B).
