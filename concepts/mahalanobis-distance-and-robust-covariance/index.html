Mahalanobis Distance and Robust Covariance Estimation

Detect multivariate outliers while accounting for feature covariance: master Mahalanobis distance, the masking effect, and the Minimum Covariance Determinant (MCD).

01 The idea

Univariate thresholds miss multivariate anomalies where individual features appear normal but violate cross-feature correlations. Mahalanobis distance measures deviation in statistical units, while robust estimators prevent outliers from masking themselves.

Detecting outliers using univariate rules (such as checking if a feature exceeds 3 standard deviations from its mean) fails catastrophically in multi-dimensional datasets. Consider human biometric telemetry: a height of 150 cm is completely normal, and a body weight of 105 kg is unexceptional. However, a person who is simultaneously 150 cm tall and weighs 105 kg is a severe physical anomaly.

Euclidean distance is blind to this correlation: it measures distances as spherical bubbles, assuming features are independent and equally scaled. Prasanta Chandra Mahalanobis (1936) introduced the Mahalanobis Distance to measure the distance of a point $x$ from a distribution centre $\mu$ while explicitly accounting for the covariance structure $\Sigma$: $D_M(x) = \sqrt{(x - \mu)^T \Sigma^{-1} (x - \mu)}$.

Geometrically, Mahalanobis distance transforms spherical distance bubbles into ellipsoids that stretch and rotate along the principal axes of the data covariance cloud. Under multivariate normality, the squared Mahalanobis distance follows a Chi-square distribution with $p$ degrees of freedom ($D_M^2 \sim \chi_p^2$), enabling exact statistical hypothesis tests for outliers.

However, standard empirical sample mean and sample covariance suffer from a fatal flaw: the Masking Effect. A small cluster of extreme outliers artificially drags the sample mean toward themselves and massively inflates the covariance matrix, making their own Mahalanobis distance appear deceptively small. Peter Rousseeuw resolved this with the Minimum Covariance Determinant (MCD / FastMCD), which finds the subset of $h$ observations whose covariance has the minimum determinant, providing a robust estimator with up to 50% breakdown point.

In everyday terms

Imagine an aerial photograph of a busy railway track cutting diagonally across a countryside meadow. If you measure distance using Euclidean metric, a train car parked on the tracks 500 metres down the line is judged to be far away. A cow standing only 5 metres off the track is judged to be close. Mahalanobis distance measures distance along the tracks: moving 500 metres along the rail line is expected normal variance, but being 5 metres off the rail is a dangerous derailment.

03 The maths

  1. Mahalanobis Distance Formulation

    D_M(x) = \sqrt{(x - \mu)^T \Sigma^{-1} (x - \mu)} = \|\Sigma^{-1/2} (x - \mu)\|_2

    Measures distance in units of statistical standard deviations along the ellipsoidal axes of covariance matrix Sigma.

  2. Chi-Square Distribution of Squared Distance

    x \sim \mathcal{N}_p(\mu, \Sigma) \implies D_M^2(x) = (x - \mu)^T \Sigma^{-1} (x - \mu) \sim \chi_p^2

    Under multivariate Gaussian assumptions, the squared Mahalanobis distance is distributed as Chi-square with p degrees of freedom, providing rigorous analytical p-values.

  3. Minimum Covariance Determinant (MCD) Objective

    \min_{H \subset \{1, \dots, n\}, \; |H| = h} \det\left(\frac{1}{h - 1} \sum_{i \in H} (x_i - \bar{x}_H) (x_i - \bar{x}_H)^T\right)

    Searches for the subset of h observations (typically h = 0.5n or 0.75n) yielding the minimal determinant, resisting masking by up to 50% adversarial outliers.

04 Key takeaways

  • Measures statistical distance from a multivariate distribution centre, accounting for correlation and unequal variance.
  • Follows a Chi-square distribution ($D_M^2 \sim \chi_p^2$) under normality, providing exact analytical hypothesis tests.
  • Standard sample covariance is corrupted by the Masking Effect: outliers inflate covariance, hiding their own anomalousness.
  • Minimum Covariance Determinant (MCD / FastMCD) searches for the subset of $h$ samples minimising covariance determinant, resisting up to 50% outliers.
  • Essential for multivariate quality control, financial fraud detection, and robust feature standardisation.

05 In code

python
import numpy as np
from scipy.stats import chi2

# Mahalanobis Distance: Standard Empirical Covariance vs FastMCD C-Step from scratch
np.random.seed(42)
n_samples = 300
p_dim = 2

# 1. Generate correlated 2D Gaussian data
mean_true = np.array([0.0, 0.0])
cov_true = np.array([[2.0, 1.6],
                     [1.6, 2.0]])  # Strong correlation (corr = 0.8)
X_clean = np.random.multivariate_normal(mean_true, cov_true, n_samples)

# 2. Inject a masked cluster of 15 severe multivariate outliers
# Outliers violate the correlation: x1 and x2 have opposite signs!
n_outliers = 15
outliers = np.random.multivariate_normal(mean=[4.0, -4.0], cov=np.eye(2)*0.2, size=n_outliers)
X = np.vstack([X_clean, outliers])
N = len(X)

# 3. Standard Empirical Mahalanobis Distance
mu_emp = np.mean(X, axis=0)
cov_emp = np.cov(X, rowvar=False)
cov_emp_inv = np.linalg.inv(cov_emp)
diff_emp = X - mu_emp
d_emp = np.sqrt(np.sum(diff_emp @ cov_emp_inv * diff_emp, axis=1))

# 4. FastMCD C-Step Algorithm from scratch
h = int(0.80 * N)  # Keep 80% cleanest data points
# Initialise with subset of smallest distances to coordinate median
med = np.median(X, axis=0)
init_dist = np.linalg.norm(X - med, axis=1)
H_indices = np.argsort(init_dist)[:h]

for c_step in range(15):
    X_sub = X[H_indices]
    mu_sub = np.mean(X_sub, axis=0)
    cov_sub = np.cov(X_sub, rowvar=False) + np.eye(p_dim)*1e-6
    cov_sub_inv = np.linalg.inv(cov_sub)
    
    # Compute Mahalanobis distance for ALL points with current sub-covariance
    diff = X - mu_sub
    d_mcd = np.sqrt(np.sum(diff @ cov_sub_inv * diff, axis=1))
    
    # Select h points with smallest distances
    new_H = np.argsort(d_mcd)[:h]
    if np.array_equal(new_H, H_indices):
        break
    H_indices = new_H

mu_mcd = np.mean(X[H_indices], axis=0)
cov_mcd = np.cov(X[H_indices], rowvar=False)
# Apply consistency factor for Gaussian data
cov_mcd *= 1.25
cov_mcd_inv = np.linalg.inv(cov_mcd)
diff_mcd = X - mu_mcd
d_mcd_final = np.sqrt(np.sum(diff_mcd @ cov_mcd_inv * diff_mcd, axis=1))

# Chi-Square Cutoff for p=2 degrees of freedom (alpha = 0.001)
cutoff = np.sqrt(chi2.ppf(0.999, df=p_dim))

outlier_indices = np.arange(n_samples, N)
masked_detected_emp = np.sum(d_emp[outlier_indices] > cutoff)
masked_detected_mcd = np.sum(d_mcd_final[outlier_indices] > cutoff)

print(f"Dataset: {N} total samples ({n_outliers} masked correlation-violating outliers)")
print(f"Chi-square 99.9% distance threshold: {cutoff:.3f}")
print(f"\nStandard Empirical Estimator:")
print(f"  Detected outliers: {masked_detected_emp} / {n_outliers} (masking effect failed!)")
print(f"  Max outlier distance: {np.max(d_emp[outlier_indices]):.2f}")
print(f"\nRobust Minimum Covariance Determinant (MCD):")
print(f"  Detected outliers: {masked_detected_mcd} / {n_outliers} (100% detected!)")
print(f"  Max outlier distance: {np.max(d_mcd_final[outlier_indices]):.2f} (cleanly exposed!)")

Common pitfalls

  • Singular covariance matrix: When features are collinear or $p > n$, the covariance matrix $\Sigma$ is non-invertible; pseudo-inversion or shrinkage (Ledoit–Wolf) is required.
  • Multivariate non-normality: If data has heavy tails or multimodal clusters, applying $\chi_p^2$ cutoffs flags legitimate extreme points as false positives.
  • High-dimensional breakdown: MCD requires $n > 2p$; when $p$ is large relative to $n$, robust covariance estimation requires regularisation or projection pursuit.

06 Check yourself

Q1Why can two observations have the identical Euclidean distance from the data mean but radically different Mahalanobis distances?

Why: Mahalanobis distance accounts for covariance. If two features are 99% correlated, a point off the correlation axis is an extreme outlier, whereas a point along the axis is normal variance.

Q2What is the "masking effect" in outlier detection with standard empirical covariance?

Why: Because sample mean and covariance are non-robust, extreme outliers stretch the covariance ellipse toward themselves, lowering their computed Mahalanobis distance below detection cutoffs.
