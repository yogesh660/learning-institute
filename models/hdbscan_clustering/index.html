HDBSCAN (Hierarchical DBSCAN)

Runs DBSCAN at every density level at once and keeps the clusters that persist longest. Handles varying densities.

01 The idea

HDBSCAN builds a hierarchy of DBSCAN-like clusterings across all values of $\varepsilon$, then picks the clusters that are most stable across that range. You set the smallest cluster size you care about; it figures out the rest.

It first measures density per point with the core distance (distance to the k-th neighbour) and defines a "mutual reachability" distance that pushes sparse points apart. A minimum spanning tree over these distances, cut at every level, gives a full hierarchy.

Small fragments below min_cluster_size are treated as points falling out of a cluster, not new clusters. Each remaining cluster gets a stability score: roughly how long (in density terms) it survives with many points. The final answer selects the most stable non-overlapping clusters, which can come from different density levels.

In everyday terms

Watching islands appear as the tide goes out: some appear early and last, others are brief sandbars. Keep the islands that last.

02 How it works

  1. Compute each point’s core distance (min_samples neighbours).
  2. Compute mutual reachability distances and build their minimum spanning tree.
  3. Sort MST edges to build a single-linkage hierarchy.
  4. Condense it: ignore splits that shed fewer than min_cluster_size points.
  5. Select clusters by stability (excess of mass); the rest is noise.

04 The maths

  1. Mutual reachability distance

    $$d_{\text{mreach}}(a, b) = \max\{\text{core}_k(a),\; \text{core}_k(b),\; d(a, b)\}$$

    Points in sparse regions get pushed away from everything, which keeps noise from bridging clusters.

  2. Cluster stability

    $$S(C) = \sum_{p \in C}\big(\lambda_p - \lambda_{\text{birth}}(C)\big), \qquad \lambda = 1/\varepsilon$$

    How much density "mass" a cluster accumulates between its birth and when its points leave.

06 When to use it

Strengths

  • Handles clusters of varying density.
  • Few, intuitive parameters.
  • Gives membership strengths and outlier scores.

Limitations

  • Slower than DBSCAN.
  • Can label many points as noise.
  • Hierarchy can be hard to explain.

Reach for it when

Exploratory clustering of real-world data, embeddings (often after UMAP), anything where density varies.

Think twice when

When every point must be assigned to a cluster.

07 In code

python
import pandas as pd
from sklearn.preprocessing import StandardScaler

df = pd.read_csv("datasets/raw/mall_customers.csv")
X = StandardScaler().fit_transform(df[["Annual_Income_k", "Spending_Score"]])
from sklearn.cluster import HDBSCAN

hdb = HDBSCAN(min_cluster_size=15).fit(X)
print("clusters:", len(set(hdb.labels_)) - (-1 in hdb.labels_), "noise:", (hdb.labels_ == -1).sum())
print("membership strength of first rows:", hdb.probabilities_[:5].round(2))

Common pitfalls

  • Setting min_cluster_size to 2 or 3 and getting hundreds of micro-clusters.

09 Check yourself

Q1What problem of DBSCAN does HDBSCAN solve?

Why: It considers all density levels and picks stable clusters from different ones.

Q2What is the primary hyperparameter in HDBSCAN that specifies the smallest grouping an analyst considers a legitimate cluster?

Why: min_cluster_size is HDBSCAN's primary intuitive parameter, setting the minimum number of observations required to form a distinct cluster rather than being treated as noise.

Q3What label does HDBSCAN assign to points deemed unclustered noise or background clutter?

Why: Following the scikit-learn density-based convention, HDBSCAN assigns label -1 to points classified as noise.

Q4What transformation is applied to density scale $\varepsilon$ to define $\lambda$ in HDBSCAN's condensed tree?

Why: In HDBSCAN, $\lambda = 1/\varepsilon$ represents inverse distance (density). As $\varepsilon$ shrinks toward zero, density $\lambda$ increases toward infinity.

Q5How is the mutual reachability distance $d_{\text{mreach}}(a, b)$ calculated between two points $a$ and $b$?

Why: Mutual reachability distance takes the maximum of point $a$'s core distance, point $b$'s core distance, and the raw distance between them. This artificially pushes points in sparse regions far apart, preventing noise from bridging clusters.

Q6What happens during the tree condensation step in HDBSCAN when a cluster splits into two components?

Why: The condensed tree ignores splits where one branch has fewer than min_cluster_size points, recording them as points gradually detaching from the cluster as density increases.

Q7How does HDBSCAN select the final flat clusters from the condensed tree under the default "eom" (Excess of Mass) method?

Why: The Excess of Mass (EOM) criterion compares parent stability $S(\text{parent})$ with the sum of children stabilities. If the children's combined stability is greater, the children are selected; otherwise the parent is retained.

Q8What does the hdb.probabilities_ attribute quantify in a fitted HDBSCAN model?

Why: hdb.probabilities_ indicates how tightly bound each point is to its assigned cluster, evaluated as $\frac{\lambda_p - \lambda_{\text{birth}}}{\lambda_{\text{death}} - \lambda_{\text{birth}}}$. Noise points have probability 0.0.

Q9If an analyst wants HDBSCAN to produce smaller, more fine-grained sub-clusters rather than broad aggregate clusters, which setting should they change?

Why: Setting cluster_selection_method="leaf" selects the finest, homogeneous leaf sub-clusters at the tips of the condensed tree rather than merging them into broad parent clusters under Excess of Mass.

Q10Points $a$ and $b$ are separated by Euclidean distance $d(a, b) = 1.8$. In a 5-nearest neighbour density estimate, point $a$ has core distance $\text{core}_5(a) = 1.2$ and point $b$ has core distance $\text{core}_5(b) = 2.5$. What is their mutual reachability distance $d_{\text{mreach}}(a, b)$?

Why: The mutual reachability distance is $\max\{\text{core}(a), \text{core}(b), d(a, b)\} = \max\{1.2, 2.5, 1.8\} = 2.5$. Because point $b$ sits in a sparser neighbourhood ($\text{core} = 2.5$), the effective distance between them is widened to 2.5.

Q11A cluster $C$ is born at density $\lambda_{\text{birth}} = 0.50$ (corresponding to $\varepsilon = 2.0$). The cluster contains 3 points whose disappearance densities are $\lambda_1 = 1.50$, $\lambda_2 = 2.00$, and $\lambda_3 = 2.50$. What is the stability $S(C) = \sum_{p \in C} (\lambda_p - \lambda_{\text{birth}})$ of this cluster?

Why: Stability measures the cumulative persistence of points in the cluster above the birth threshold: $(1.50 - 0.50) + (2.00 - 0.50) + (2.50 - 0.50) = 1.00 + 1.50 + 2.00 = 4.50$.

Q12In the condensed tree of an HDBSCAN model, a parent cluster has stability $S(\text{parent}) = 8.5$. At a higher density, the parent splits into two child clusters with stabilities $S(\text{child}_1) = 4.0$ and $S(\text{child}_2) = 5.2$. Under the Excess of Mass (EOM) selection algorithm, which cluster(s) are selected?

Why: EOM compares the parent's stability against the sum of the children's stabilities: $S(\text{children}) = 4.0 + 5.2 = 9.2 > 8.5$. Because the children accumulate more persistence mass, the algorithm selects the two children.
