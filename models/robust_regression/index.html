Robust Regression (Huber, RANSAC, Theil–Sen)

Linear fits that refuse to be bullied by outliers, using a gentler loss or by ignoring points that do not fit.

01 The idea

OLS squares residuals, so a single bad point can take over. Robust regression changes the rules: Huber caps how hard outliers can pull, RANSAC fits only to points that agree, and Theil–Sen takes a median of slopes.

Huber uses squared loss for small residuals and absolute loss beyond a threshold $\delta$. Near the line it behaves like OLS; far away, an outlier’s pull stops growing.

RANSAC (random sample consensus) repeatedly fits a line to a tiny random subset, counts how many points lie close to it, and keeps the line with the largest consensus. Outliers are simply never in the winning subset.

Theil–Sen computes the slope between every pair of points and takes the median. Up to about 29% of the data can be garbage before the estimate breaks.

In everyday terms

Asking a room for a consensus: Huber lets loud people speak but caps their volume; RANSAC listens only to the largest group that agrees; Theil–Sen takes the median opinion.

02 How it works

  1. Choose the strategy: down-weight (Huber), exclude (RANSAC), or median-based (Theil–Sen).
  2. Huber: solve iteratively, re-weighting points by how large their residual is (IRLS).
  3. RANSAC: sample minimal subsets, fit, count inliers within a residual threshold, keep the best, refit on its inliers.
  4. Inspect which points were flagged; they are often the most interesting part of the data.

04 The maths

  1. Huber loss

    $$L_\delta(r) = \begin{cases} \tfrac12 r^2 & |r| \le \delta \\ \delta\big(|r| - \tfrac12\delta\big) & |r| > \delta \end{cases}$$

    Its derivative (the "pull" a point exerts) is $r$ inside the threshold and capped at $\pm\delta$ outside it.

  2. RANSAC iterations needed

    $$N = \frac{\log(1 - p)}{\log\big(1 - w^s\big)}$$

    To find an all-inlier sample with probability $p$ when a fraction $w$ of points are inliers and each sample has $s$ points. For a line ($s = 2$), $w = 0.6$, $p = 0.99$: $N \approx 11$.

  3. Theil–Sen slope

    $$\hat\beta_1 = \operatorname{median}_{i<j} \frac{y_j - y_i}{x_j - x_i}$$

    The median of all pairwise slopes, then the intercept is the median of $y_i - \hat\beta_1 x_i$.

05 Hyperparameters

06 When to use it

Strengths

  • Resistant to outliers and gross errors.
  • RANSAC also tells you which points are outliers.
  • Theil–Sen needs no tuning.

Limitations

  • Less efficient than OLS on perfectly clean data.
  • RANSAC is random; results vary between runs without a seed.
  • Theil–Sen gets slow with many rows or features.

Reach for it when

Sensor data, manual entry errors, or any data where a few points are plainly wrong.

Think twice when

The "outliers" are real and important (fraud, failures). Then you need to model them, not ignore them.

07 In code

python
from sklearn.linear_model import HuberRegressor, RANSACRegressor, TheilSenRegressor, LinearRegression

models = {
    "OLS": LinearRegression(),
    "Huber": HuberRegressor(epsilon=1.35),
    "RANSAC": RANSACRegressor(random_state=0),
    "Theil-Sen": TheilSenRegressor(random_state=0),
}
for name, m in models.items():
    m.fit(X_tr, y_tr)
    print(name, round(m.score(X_te, y_te), 3))

inliers = models["RANSAC"].inlier_mask_   # which training rows RANSAC trusted

Common pitfalls

  • Using robust regression to hide data-quality problems instead of investigating them.
  • Forgetting to scale before Huber; epsilon is defined relative to the residual scale.

09 Check yourself

Q1How does the Huber loss limit an outlier’s influence?

Why: Beyond $\delta$ the loss grows linearly, so its derivative stays constant: an outlier pulls with fixed force no matter how far away it is.

Q2RANSAC’s final model is fitted on:

Why: After finding the best minimal sample, RANSAC refits on all points within the residual threshold of it.

Q3How does the Theil–Sen estimator compute the slope for a simple univariate regression?

Why: Theil–Sen calculates pairwise slopes $s_{ij} = (y_j - y_i) / (x_j - x_i)$ for all $i < j$ and takes their median, making it highly robust against outliers in the dependent variable.

Q4What is the breakdown point of an estimator in robust statistics?

Why: The breakdown point measures resistance to data corruption; OLS has a breakdown point of $0\%$ (a single outlier can corrupt the slope arbitrarily), whereas Theil–Sen reaches $\approx 29.3\%$ for simple regression and RANSAC can tolerate over $50\%$ contamination.

Q5Why does Ordinary Least Squares (OLS) have a breakdown point of 0%, whereas Huber regression is more robust?

Why: In OLS, the gradient is proportional to the residual $r$; as $|r| \to \infty$, the gradient pulls with unbounded force. Huber's loss transitions to linear growth for $|r| > \delta$, capping its influence at $\pm\delta$.

Q6What is a key vulnerability of Huber regression when dealing with leverage points (outliers in the feature space $X$)?

Why: The loss gradient with respect to weight $\beta$ is $\sum x_i L_\delta'(r_i)$. Even though $L_\delta'(r_i)$ is bounded by $\delta$, extreme $x_i$ can make the product arbitrarily large. High-breakdown estimators like RANSAC handle high-leverage points much more effectively.

Q7Why must residuals be scaled properly before setting the parameter $\epsilon$ (or $\delta$) in Huber regression?

Why: The threshold $\delta$ defines where the loss switches from quadratic to linear. In HuberRegressor(epsilon=1.35), $\delta = 1.35 \times \hat{\sigma}$; if residual scale $\hat{\sigma}$ is misestimated, the threshold cannot distinguish normal noise from genuine outliers.

Q8In RANSAC, what is the role of the residual threshold parameter?

Why: RANSAC classifies any point with residual $|y_i - \hat{y}_i| \le \text{threshold}$ as an inlier supporting the candidate model. A threshold set too tight rejects true inliers; set too loose, it admits outliers into the consensus set.

Q9How does Iteratively Reweighted Least Squares (IRLS) solve the Huber regression problem?

Why: The Huber loss can be viewed as weighted least squares where the effective weight is $w_i = 1$ when $|r_i| \le \delta$ and $w_i = \delta / |r_i|$ when $|r_i| > \delta$. Iteratively updating weights and refitting weighted least squares converges to the Huber minimum.

Q10A Huber loss function uses threshold $\delta = 2.0$. For an observation with true target $y = 15.0$ and prediction $\hat{y} = 9.0$, what is the exact Huber loss $L_\delta(r)$ and the magnitude of its derivative $|L'_\delta(r)|$?

Why: The residual is $r = 15.0 - 9.0 = 6.0$. Since $|r| = 6.0 > \delta = 2.0$, we use the linear branch: $L_\delta(r) = \delta(|r| - \frac{1}{2}\delta) = 2.0 \times (6.0 - 1.0) = 10.0$. The derivative magnitude is capped at $\delta = 2.0$.

Q11You are fitting a line (minimal sample size $s = 2$ points) using RANSAC. Outliers make up 40% of the dataset (inlier ratio $w = 0.60$). What is the minimum number of iterations $N = \frac{\log(1 - p)}{\log(1 - w^s)}$ needed to guarantee finding at least one outlier-free sample with probability $p = 0.99$? (Use $\log(0.01) \approx -4.605$ and $\log(1 - 0.60^2) = \log(0.64) \approx -0.446$).

Why: The probability of picking two inliers is $w^2 = 0.60^2 = 0.36$. The probability of picking at least one contaminated point is $1 - 0.36 = 0.64$. To ensure $(0.64)^N \le 0.01$, $N \ge \frac{-4.605}{-0.446} \approx 10.32$. Rounding up gives $N = 11$ iterations.

Q12Consider 5 data points: $(1, 2), (2, 4), (3, 6), (4, 8)$ and an outlier $(5, 50)$. What is the Theil–Sen slope estimate $\hat{\beta}_1$, and what is the OLS slope?

Why: There are $\binom{5}{2} = 10$ pairs. The 4 inliers form $\binom{4}{2} = 6$ pairs that all have slope $(y_j - y_i)/(x_j - x_i) = 2.0$. In the sorted list of 10 slopes, positions 1 through 6 are all $2.0$, giving a median of $2.0$. In contrast, OLS minimises squared error and yields slope $\frac{\sum(x - \bar{x})(y - \bar{y})}{\sum(x - \bar{x})^2} = \frac{100}{10} = 10.0$, heavily corrupted by the single outlier.
